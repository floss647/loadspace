These are the UK tax and grant rules our [calculators](/tools/) use for 2026/27. Check your own position with your accountant.

### Road tax

Most vans up to 3,500kg, diesel, petrol or electric, are £360 a year from 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027; some older Euro 4 and Euro 5 vans pay less. Most electric vans moved onto the standard van rate on 1 April 2025, so they pay the same as diesel.

### The van grant

The Zero Emission Van Grant takes up to 35% off the price of an eligible new electric van, to a maximum of £2,500 for a small van (under 2,500kg gross vehicle weight) or £5,000 for a large van (2,500kg to 4,250kg). You don't apply for it: the dealer takes it off the price. Only listed models qualify, and there are a limited number of grants.

### Company vans: the driver's tax

If an employee can use a company diesel or petrol van privately, they pay income tax on a van benefit charge of £4,170, at their own rate: 20% basic, 40% higher or 45% additional rate (Scotland's bands differ). If the business also pays for private fuel, a van fuel benefit charge of £798 is added. Both are nil (£0) for a zero-emission van.

There's no charge if the van is mainly for business travel, is used privately only for commuting and the terms of use forbid other private use (HMRC calls this the restricted private use condition). Insignificant private use, such as a small detour for a newspaper on the way to work, doesn't break that condition.

### Buying or leasing a van

The Annual Investment Allowance lets a business deduct the full cost of equipment from profits, up to £1 million a year. Diesel and electric vans both qualify; cars don't. HMRC's guidance says the allowance covers virtually all plant and machinery, including vans, subject only to a short list of exclusions; being second-hand isn't one of them, so a used van can qualify too. Limited companies can instead claim full expensing, which deducts 100% of the cost, but only on new and unused vans. Companies, sole traders and partnerships can also claim a 40% first-year allowance on new and unused vans bought on or after 1 January 2026. Sole traders and partnerships using cash basis claim a van's cost as an allowable expense instead. If you sell a van after claiming the allowance, you may need to pay tax.

The 100% first-year allowance for zero-emission goods vehicles only covered vans bought before April 2025, so electric vans now get the same relief as diesel.

For sole traders and partners, gov.uk lists vehicle hire charges as an allowable business expense. Limited companies should check how van lease rentals are treated with their accountant.

### Tax rates on profits

Companies pay corporation tax at 19% on profits up to £50,000 and 25% on profits over £250,000. In between, marginal relief applies, making the rate on each extra pound 26.5%. The £50,000 and £250,000 limits are reduced for short accounting periods and for companies with associated companies.

Sole traders and partners pay income tax plus Class 4 National Insurance of 6% on profits between £12,570 and £50,270 and 2% above that. So the rate on each extra pound is 26% for basic-rate, 42% for higher-rate and 47% for additional-rate taxpayers. Scotland's bands differ, and the Personal Allowance is reduced on income over £100,000, which raises the rate between £100,000 and £125,140.

### Double-cab pickups

From April 2025, HMRC expects most double-cab pickups to count as cars for capital allowances and company vehicle tax: no Annual Investment Allowance or full expensing, and the car benefit charge instead of the van charge. Transitional rules: for capital allowances, pickups with a payload of one tonne or more bought under contracts made before 1 April 2025 (6 April for income tax) with spending before 1 October 2025; for company vehicle tax, pickups bought, leased or ordered before 6 April 2025 keep the van treatment until they're sold, the lease ends or 5 April 2029, whichever comes first.

### Budget 2026: changes not yet in force

- From 1 January 2027, the first-year allowance on new vans is due to rise from 40% to 50%, subject to Finance Bill 2026-27 becoming law.
- From 1 April 2027, van road tax is due to rise to £375 a year, subject to the same Finance Bill.
- In the 2027 to 2028 tax year, the van benefit charge is due to rise to £4,310 and the van fuel benefit charge to £825.
