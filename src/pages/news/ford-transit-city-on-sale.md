Ford's **Transit City** is an **electric only** van that Van Reviewer reports is close in size to a Transit Custom, but much cheaper. It isn't brand new: Ford revealed it in March 2026, and its UK price list is dated 29 April 2026. Van Reviewer reported at the reveal that order books would open in early Q2 2026, with first UK deliveries most likely from October to November.

Ford's price list starts it at **£29,000 before VAT**, or **£29,436** before VAT with on-the-road costs. That's **£14,630 less** than the cheapest E-Transit Custom, which Ford lists from £43,630 before VAT. Both prices are before the van grant.

The van is **made in Nanchang, China**, by **Ford's Chinese partner JMC**, and the **batteries come from CATL**, Van Reviewer reports. It also reports that, to save cost, there's just one trim. Ford's price list does still list some paid options, such as metallic paint at £650 before VAT. Van Reviewer calls the **56kWh** pack a relatively small battery for a van this size.

## The key numbers

|---|---|---|
| List price, before VAT | £29,000 | £31,500 |
| On-the-road price, before VAT | £29,436 | £31,936 |
| Official range (WLTP combined) | 158 miles | 139 miles |
| Load volume | 6.4m³ | 8.5m³ |
| Payload | 1,085kg | 1,275kg |
| Gross weight | 3,000kg | 3,270kg |

Figures are from Ford UK's website and price list. Prices can change, so check with a dealer.

- **Battery:** 56kWh of usable capacity, using LFP (lithium iron phosphate) cells.
- **Range in town:** Ford quotes up to 236.7 miles for the short van on the WLTP city test. Real-world range will be lower in cold weather or on faster roads.
- **Motor:** 110kW (150PS) and 260Nm, driving the front wheels. Auto Express reports 0-62mph in 13.7 seconds and a top speed of 75mph.
- **Charging:**
  - **Rapid charging:** up to 87kW. Ford says 10–80% takes 33 minutes.
  - **Depot charging:** 11kW three-phase takes 5.5 hours for 10–100%, according to Ford. On a 7.4kW single-phase charger, allow 8 hours.
- **Kit:** a 12.3-inch touchscreen with Apple CarPlay and Android Auto (connected by USB), air conditioning, a heated driver's seat, keyless start, front and rear parking sensors, a reversing camera and adaptive cruise control.
- **Warranty:** three years or 100,000 miles on the van, according to Ford's price list. Trade press reports an eight-year, 100,000-mile (160,000km) warranty on the high-voltage parts.
- **Servicing:** every two years, with no mileage limit, according to Ford's price list.
- **Towing:** Van Reviewer reports it can't tow. If you pull a trailer, this isn't your van.

## Bodies and conversions

There are two panel vans. A chassis cab is on the price list too, at £31,500 before VAT, and Ford says it can carry up to 1,600kg. Auto Express reports that tipper and Luton-style conversions will come from existing converters, and Van Reviewer reports a refrigerated body is due from early 2027.

## Does it get the van grant?

Yes. GOV.UK lists the **Ford Transit City Van**, and the chassis cab, as large vans for the [Zero Emission Van Grant](/news/zero-emission-van-grant-explained/), worth up to £5,000. Large vans must weigh between 2,500kg and 4,250kg fully loaded, and both Transit City vans fall in that band. You don't claim it yourself: the seller includes the discount in the price. Ford's UK website advertises the £5,000 grant saving.

Ford's website currently shows the short van from **£22,200**. Ford's small print says that figure is before VAT and on-the-road costs, and includes the £5,000 grant plus a Ford customer saving for VAT-registered businesses that keep the van for at least nine months. Taking £5,000 off the £29,000 list price gives £24,000, which suggests the customer saving is about £1,800. Ask your dealer for a written quote that shows each saving and the VAT clearly.

## What it means for you

The Transit City is built for **local work**: urban delivery, couriers and trades who can charge at home or at the depot.

- **The compromise is range.** 158 miles is the official figure for the short van, and the long van is rated lower.
- **Need to tow or do long motorway days?** Ford says the [E-Transit Custom](/vans/ford-transit-custom/) can tow 2.3 tonnes, and Van Reviewer reports it has a bigger battery.
- **We haven't driven one yet.** We'll add our impressions to our [Transit City page](/vans/ford-transit-city/) when we have.
