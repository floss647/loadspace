The government's review of the **zero-emission vehicle (ZEV) mandate**, the rule that makes van makers sell a rising share of electric vans, closed on **23 October 2026**. The consultation opened on 14 August and ran for 10 weeks.

Run jointly by the UK and devolved governments, it asks whether the yearly targets are still realistic. **No decision has been announced yet.**

## How the target works

Each year, a set share of every maker's new van registrations must be zero emission. For vans, the target started at 10% in 2024 and rises to 70% in 2030, according to the consultation document. This year it is **24%**. Under current rules it then climbs to 34% in 2027, 46% in 2028 and 58% in 2029.

Makers don't have to hit those figures through sales alone, at least until 2029. They can:

- **Earn credits** by cutting CO2 emissions from their non-electric vans
- **Borrow** against future years, or **bank** spare credits for later
- **Move credits** between their car and van totals, or buy them from other makers

A maker that still falls short pays **£15,000 per van**, down from £18,000 after changes announced in April 2025.

## Where sales are

Sales are well behind. Zero-emission vans made up 6.8% of new vans in 2024 and 9.7% in 2025, against targets of 10% and 16%. The 2025 figure is provisional.

The van market still complied overall, thanks to the flexibilities. With them counted, it reached the equivalent of 12.1% in 2024, and the government expects 16.9% for 2025.

Between January and September this year, battery electric vans took **10.8%** of the market, according to SMMT, against the 24% target. More in our [September sales story](/news/record-september-electric-van-sales-2026/).

The government's own modelling says electric van sales would need to grow by an average of 52% a year between 2025 and 2030 to hit the 2030 target, assuming no flexibilities are used that year. That compares with 18% a year from 2022 to 2025.

## What's on the table

Alongside keeping things as they are, the consultation sets out four alternatives for vans:

- **Cut the target to 60% in 2030**
- **Cut it to 50% in 2030**
- **Cut it to 40% in 2030**, then rise more steeply after that. Under this option, the 2027 target would be 28%.
- **Keep 70%**, but extend borrowing, banking and the CO2 credit flexibility to 2034. They currently end in 2029.

The three cuts would all start from 2027. Every option still reaches 100% by 2035. The government says it will not change that end date, when all new vans must be zero emission. It also asked for other ideas, so the final answer may not match any of these exactly.

Trucks aren't part of this review. A separate consultation on rules for HGVs closed on 17 March 2026.

## Who wants what

- **SMMT**, the motor industry body, welcomed the review. Its chief executive Mike Hawes said: "Regulatory targets are now running ahead of current consumer demand, so this review is a timely opportunity to optimise the pace of change." As the consultation closed, SMMT said makers can't keep making up the shortfall by discounting forever. It wants more realistic targets, better flexibilities, incentives to boost demand and faster rollout of charging.
- **Ford wants any changes confirmed in 2026**, so the industry and customers have time to prepare, according to SMMT.
- **Stellantis says it met the mandate in 2024 and 2025**, but that compliance is coming at an increasingly significant and unsustainable cost.
- **BVRLA**, the leasing and rental body, said: "Any review needs to be grounded in evidence and the experience of those delivering the transition every day."

## What it means for you

- **Nothing changes yet.** The current targets apply until the government publishes its decision. It hasn't said when that will be.
- **Discounts may not last forever.** SMMT says substantial discounts helped drive record electric van sales in September. Much of that discounting is makers trying to meet the mandate. If the targets are cut, that pressure could ease.
