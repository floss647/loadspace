The UK's new truck market grew in the third quarter of 2026, for the first time in two years. Zero-emission truck registrations halved.

## The numbers

According to figures published by the motor industry body SMMT on 12 October, **9,941 new heavy goods vehicles** were registered between July and September, up **7.2%** on the same period last year. It was the first quarter of growth since Q2 2024.

SMMT says the rise is flattered by a weak comparison: registrations had fallen 14.5% in the third quarter of 2025. Across the first nine months of 2026, the market is still down 3.8%.

- **Articulated trucks** rose 11.2% to 4,543.
- **Rigid trucks** rose 4.1% to 5,398. Rigids over 16 tonnes were up 4.6%, and those from 6 to 16 tonnes up 3.1%.

Zero-emission truck registrations fell 50.7%. They made up just **1.1%** of the market, against 2.4% a year earlier.

SMMT doesn't give a unit total for this quarter. A year earlier, 225 zero-emission trucks were registered, then a record, so this year's figure works out at roughly 110 by our sums. So far in 2026, zero-emission registrations are down 30.9%, with a 1.0% share.

"A recovery in new HGV registrations after two years of falling demand is a welcome boost for the sector, albeit from a low base," said Mike Hawes, SMMT's chief executive. "The decline in zero-emission registrations, however, underlines the volatility and fragility of the transition in a sector with complex operating requirements, with market share still stuck at just one in 100 new trucks."

## Why electric trucks fell

SMMT doesn't single out one cause, but says the cost of buying the trucks and building depot charging "remains a significant barrier", lowered only in part by government support. It also wants a national infrastructure strategy to give operators confidence.

The comparison is also tough. Last year's third quarter was unusually strong: zero-emission uptake more than quadrupled.

Government-backed trials matter in a market this small. The ZEHID programme (Zero Emission HGV and Infrastructure Demonstrator) has deployed more than 240 zero-emission trucks and 40 charging sites, trans.info reports. One of its projects, Electric Freightway, accounted for 24% of all new electric HGV registrations in the UK in 2025. The programme hasn't ended: around 300 trucks and 70 sites are expected by the end of 2026, and monitoring runs until 2031.

The same report found the funded electric trucks cost around 2.7 times as much as diesel ones to buy. Charging is slow to arrive too: one project site was quoted five years for a grid connection.

## Help available

**Zero Emission Truck Grant.** The seller takes the discount off the price. According to GOV.UK, the maximum is a share of the price or a cash cap, whichever is lower:

- 4.25 to 12 tonnes: 20%, up to £15,000
- 12 to 18 tonnes: 40%, up to £37,000
- 18 to 26 tonnes: 40%, up to £52,000
- Over 26 tonnes: 40%, up to £81,000

In Northern Ireland, large firms get up to 30% rather than 40% on the heavier trucks. Only approved models qualify.

**Depot Charging Scheme.** Businesses can get up to 70% of the cost of installing chargers for vans, coaches and HGVs, capped at £1 million, after a £170 million boost announced in March. See our [depot charging guide](/guides/depot-charging/).

## The deadlines

The government plans to end sales of new non-zero-emission HGVs up to and including 26 tonnes by 2035, and of all new non-zero-emission HGVs by 2040. A consultation on the rules to get there ran from 6 January to 17 March 2026. The government says it is analysing the feedback. No response has been published yet.

## What it means for operators

- **Check the grant on your quote.** The cap depends on weight, and the lightest trucks get a smaller share.
- **Start the power conversation early.** Grid connections can take years.
- **Be realistic about mileage.** In early use, ZEHID's electric trucks averaged about 1,350 km a week (about 840 miles), against about 2,600 km (about 1,600 miles) for diesel trucks, although the report says the operations are not directly comparable.
