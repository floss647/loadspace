Whether an electric van saves a small business money depends heavily on where it charges, according to modelling reported by Greenfleet. The research was carried out by the Centre for Economics and Business Research (Cebr) and commissioned by the **Open Road Partnership**: United Rental Group, Zenith, Enterprise Mobility and Vertu Motors.

Cebr looked at three illustrative businesses, including **a construction firm running 10 vans** and **a sole-trader window cleaner**, and compared electric with diesel over five years. Its sums included purchase and finance, insurance, charging equipment, tax, grants, fuel and electricity, servicing, and the time lost using public chargers.

## The 10-van business

- **Year one costs more.** Choosing electric could cost the business **£15,500** extra in the year of purchase, mainly because of the up-front cost of the vans and chargers.
- **With its own charging, it pays back.** With access to private charging, annual costs fall below diesel from 2027, the business is expected to break even by 2028, and it saves **around £42,000** over five years.
- **Relying on public charging, it doesn't.** The same fleet could pay **£110,000 more** over five years, including the cost of lost working time. That works out at more than **£23,800** a year, as vans and staff spend working time getting to and using public chargers. The gap between the two approaches could reach **£150,000**.

## The sole trader

For the window cleaner, financing an electric van would leave the business **around £100 worse off** after five years than buying a diesel van outright. Cebr's analysis suggests financing can wipe out the longer-term savings, even though it spreads the up-front cost.

## What the partnership wants

The partnership is calling on the government to use its review of the ZEV Mandate, the rule setting how many new cars and vans must be electric, to set "a realistic and achievable trajectory". It also wants faster access to affordable, reliable charging and help with depot charging, plus more time and flexibility for small firms to switch.

Per Voegerl, chief executive of United Rental Group, said that for many small and medium businesses "the economics of making the switch simply don't stack up yet".

These are modelled examples, not measurements of real firms, and they come from companies with a stake in how the transition is run. Your own numbers will depend on your mileage, your vans and where you can charge.

## What it means for you

- **Charging is the big question.** If you can charge at home or at a yard, the case for electric looks much stronger in this modelling. If you'd rely on public chargers, count the time as well as the cost.
- **Check the depot grant.** The [Depot Charging Scheme](/news/depot-charging-scheme-second-window/) is open for applications until February.
