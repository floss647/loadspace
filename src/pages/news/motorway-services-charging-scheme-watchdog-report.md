The government wants to pay for **electricity grid upgrades at 15 to 25 motorway service areas in England**, so they can host far more chargers for cars, vans, trucks, coaches and motorbikes. The plan, called the **Strategic Charging Infrastructure Scheme**, has an indicative budget of **£190 million**.

The scheme is proposed by the Office for Zero Emission Vehicles (OZEV). Because individual subsidies are expected to top £1 million, OZEV asked the Subsidy Advice Unit, part of the Competition and Markets Authority, to look at it. OZEV made that request on 24 September, and the unit published its report on **4 November**. The report is non-binding advice: it doesn't approve or block the scheme, but it tells OZEV what it still needs to show.

## How the scheme would work

- **The government pays for the grid work.** OZEV would commission the upgrades at chosen sites, then reserve the extra power for service area operators or chargepoint companies to put in public chargers open to any electric vehicle.
- **Firms still pay something.** They'd pay a charge to use the reserved capacity, which OZEV would partly waive to make the sums work. They also pay for the chargers themselves and the rest of the on-site kit.
- **The reservation won't last forever.** OZEV currently plans to hold the capacity for three to five years. Anything not taken up by then goes to any energy user, without the subsidy.
- **Sites are picked on need.** OZEV would choose places where the gap between today's capacity and future needs is biggest, and where upgrading the grid isn't commercially viable. The aim is to meet expected charging demand at those sites until at least 2035, and in some cases until 2050.
- **The money** comes from the £400 million set aside in Spending Review 2025 for charging between 2026 and 2030.

## What the watchdog's advisers said

The report says OZEV has clearly described and evidenced what the scheme is for, and weighed its benefits against its downsides in detail. But it asks OZEV to do more work in several areas, including:

- showing the scheme brings **extra investment**, and how it will leave out sites where investment would have happened anyway;
- checking it has ways to make sure the money actually adds charging capacity;
- looking harder at the effect on competition, including on nearby charging sites away from motorway services.

Some key details still aren't settled: the exact size of the subsidy, the cap on connection costs it will cover, and how long the capacity will be reserved. The report says OZEV can't fully show the scheme meets the rules until those are decided.

## What it means for you

If your vans do long motorway days, this is aimed at your biggest worry: finding a working charger at the services. One of OZEV's stated aims is a visible and reliable long-distance charging network.

Truck operators are included too. OZEV's own assessment, quoted in the report, says electric HGV charging is at an early stage, with fewer than ten public locations.

**Nothing changes yet.** The scheme is still a proposal, and the report doesn't name any sites or give a start date. We'll report when OZEV confirms the final design.
