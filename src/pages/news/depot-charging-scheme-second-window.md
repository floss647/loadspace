The government's **Depot Charging Scheme** has opened a second round, with **£38 million** to help fleets put electric vehicle chargers in at their depots, Greenfleet reports. The window opened at midday on 11 November, two weeks later than planned, and closes at **midday on 26 February 2027**. SMMT also refers to the £38 million window.

## What you can get

- **Up to 60%** of eligible charge point and civil engineering costs. That's down from 70% in the first round, according to Greenfleet.
- **Up to £1 million** per organisation in total, across all your sites.
- **Who can apply:** public and private sector fleets, local authorities and non-profit organisations across the UK. The scheme is aimed at HGVs, vans and coaches.
- **How:** one application per organisation in each window, but it can include any number of sites. Applications are first come, first served, unless demand outstrips the money, when a competitive process may be used.
- **Deadline to finish:** projects must be completed by 31 March 2028.

Greenfleet reports that further windows are expected in the 2028/29 and 2029/30 financial years, with timings and grant rates due to be confirmed in 2027.

## The catch

SMMT welcomed the money, but warned that a charger is only useful if there is ready electricity supply. It said grid capacity and connections must urgently be treated as central to the policy, rather than left as an administrative headache for operators. It said making the business case work is tougher for smaller firms, and that a van returning to base each evening needs different charging from a long-distance HGV. SMMT said future grant rates are likely to fall as the switch to electric gathers pace, though for the heaviest vehicles that is some time away.

## What it means for you

- **Running a few vans from a yard?** If your project qualifies, the scheme pays up to 60% of eligible charger and civil engineering costs, so you'd still pay at least 40% yourself. SMMT notes that making the business case work is tougher for smaller firms.
- **Timing.** Applications are handled first come, first served unless demand outstrips the £38 million, when a competitive process may be used. The window closes at midday on 26 February 2027.
