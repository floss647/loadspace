The UK had **124,738 public chargers** on 1 October 2026, according to Department for Transport figures published on 25 November. Of those, **29,772** were rated rapid or above (50kW and up), almost a quarter of the network.

## The power bands

The Department for Transport splits the network into four power bands. On 1 October 2026:

| Band | Power | Share of public chargers |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | 3kW to less than 8kW | 49% |
| Standard plus | 8kW to less than 50kW | 27% |
| Rapid | 50kW to less than 150kW | 12% |
| Ultra-rapid | 150kW and above | 12% |

In our view, the chargers rated rapid or above are the ones that can give a van a big top-up during a short stop.

## On the main roads

The latest release adds a new figure to the series. On 1 October 2026, England had **9,616** public chargers rated rapid or above within a mile of its motorways and major A-roads, measured by driving route from junctions. Of those, 8,857 were "open-access", meaning their connectors are not limited to specific vehicle makes.

The Department for Transport notes that "open-access" tells you nothing about the landowner's permission, opening hours or how accessible a charger is. In our view, it's worth checking a site before you rely on it.

Across the UK, 11% of public chargers were classed as en-route (for continuing a journey), which includes those at motorway service areas and rapid or above chargers at hospitality venues. Just over half (52%) were destination chargers, such as those in car parks, and a third (33%) were on-street chargers.

## Chargers added

According to the Department for Transport, the network grew by 8,686 public chargers in 2026 up to 1 October, after growing by 13,281 in the whole of 2025.

## Where the chargers are

Where you work matters. The Department for Transport says London has many more public chargers per person than the rest of the UK, but the second-lowest number of chargers rated rapid or above per person of any UK nation or region, because much of its network is standard (3kW to less than 8kW) on-street chargers.

## About the figures

The figures come from charge point operators and are collated by Zapmap. The Department for Transport publishes them as official statistics in development, meaning the series is still being developed and tested with users. The next update is due in February 2027.
