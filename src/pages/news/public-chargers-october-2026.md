The UK had **124,738 public chargers** on 1 October 2026, according to the latest Department for Transport figures. Of those, **29,772** were rated rapid or above (50kW and up), almost a quarter of the network.

## How fast the chargers are

The Department for Transport splits the network into four power bands:

| Band | Power | Share of public chargers |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | 3kW to less than 8kW | 49% |
| Standard plus | 8kW to less than 50kW | 27% |
| Rapid | 50kW to less than 150kW | 12% |
| Ultra-rapid | 150kW and above | 12% |

For a working van that needs a top-up during the day, it's the rapid and ultra-rapid chargers that count.

## On the main roads

For the first time the figures have included this: England had **9,616** rapid or faster public chargers within a mile of England's motorways and major A-roads, measured by driving route from the junction. Of those, 8,857 were "open-access", meaning the connector is not limited to one make of car. The Department for Transport notes that "open-access" doesn't say anything about opening hours or access, so it's worth checking a site before you rely on it.

Across the UK, 11% of public chargers are classed as en-route chargers, which includes those at motorway services. Most are destination chargers (52%), such as car parks, or on-street chargers (33%).

## How quickly it's growing

8,686 public chargers were added between 1 January and 1 October 2026. In the whole of 2025, 13,281 were added.

Where you work matters. The Department for Transport says London has many more public chargers per person than the rest of the UK, but the second-lowest number of rapid chargers per person, because so many of its chargers are slower on-street points.

The data comes from charge point operators, collated by Zapmap. The Department for Transport publishes them as official statistics in development, and the next update is due in February 2027.
