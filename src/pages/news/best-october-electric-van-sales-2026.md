SMMT's figures, published on 5 November, show **25,510 new vans, pickups and 4x4s** were registered in October, up **11.4%** on last October. It's the seventh month of growth in a row, and the market is up 4.8% so far this year at 276,885.

Electric vans had their best October ever: **3,369 registrations, up 58.0%** from 2,132 a year earlier. That took them to **12.9%** of the market, up from 9.2% last October. The share is 3,369 out of 26,098 registrations, according to SMMT, whose total counts the heavier electric vans too (see below).

So far this year the electric share is **11.0%**, up from 10.8% after September, against the **24%** that the Zero Emission Vehicle Mandate requires this year.

SMMT's chief executive, Mike Hawes, said: "Seven months of new van market growth reflects a slight recovery in buyer confidence after 2025, but the fragility of this confidence makes the EV transition particularly tough to deliver. While electric van uptake is accelerating rapidly, demand remains far behind the optimistic and unrealistic expectations of the mandate."

## The numbers

All figures are from SMMT.

- **Large vans** (2.5 to 3.5 tonnes): 18,560, up 12.9%. That's 72.8% of the market.
- **Medium vans** (2.0 to 2.5 tonnes): 3,825, up 14.3%.
- **Small vans** (up to 2.0 tonnes): 462, down 11.7%.
- **Pickups:** 1,764, down 17.7%. So far this year they're down 52.9% on the same period in 2025, which SMMT links to the tax change that treats double-cab pickups as cars for benefit-in-kind and capital allowances.
- **4x4s:** 899, up 104.3%.
- **Best sellers:** the Ford Transit Custom (2,963), Mercedes-Benz Sprinter (2,020) and Ford Transit (1,863). These counts cover all fuel types, not just electric.

SMMT's electric total includes heavier electric vans of 3.5 to 4.25 tonnes, which count towards makers' targets. These rose from 182 to 588. Electric vans up to 3.5 tonnes grew by 42.6% to 2,781.

So far this year, 30,728 electric vans have been registered, up 26.7%.

## Why it's still behind

There's no shortage of choice: SMMT says more than 40 electric van models are on sale, and that makers have been forced into heavy discounts to avoid even bigger costs under the mandate.

SMMT doesn't expect the gap to close. Its latest outlook has electric vans up to 3.5 tonnes taking 10.4% of the market this year, 13.6 percentage points short of the target. It forecasts 14.9% in 2027 and 18.8% in 2028, against targets of 34% and 46%, with the gap only widening.

SMMT says the government's review of the mandate must bring significant change. It also wants a long-term commitment to purchase incentives, much faster rollout of public charging suited to vans, and shorter waits for depot grid connections.
