September is always a big month for new vans because of the new number plate. SMMT's figures, published on 5 October, show **49,704 new vans, pickups and 4x4s** were registered, up **4.8%** on last September. That's the highest monthly total of 2026 and the sixth month of growth in a row, according to SMMT.

Electric vans had their best month ever: **4,832 registrations, up 13.4%** from 4,262 a year earlier. But that's still only **9.6%** of the market, up from 8.9% last September. So far this year the share is **10.8%**, against the **24%** that the Zero Emission Vehicle Mandate requires this year.

SMMT's chief executive, Mike Hawes, said: "Yet, even with high fuel prices, demand remains less than half the mandated level due to higher purchase costs, grid delays and a shortage of van-suitable charging."

## The numbers

All figures are from SMMT.

- **Large vans** (2.5 to 3.5 tonnes): 36,302, up 11.1%.
- **Medium vans** (2.0 to 2.5 tonnes): 7,304, up 2.2%.
- **Small vans** (up to 2.0 tonnes): 1,361, up 13.2%.
- **Pickups:** 2,658, down 53.8%. SMMT links the fall to the tax change that treats double-cab pickups as cars for benefit-in-kind and capital allowances.
- **4x4s:** 2,079, more than three times last September's 647, which SMMT puts down to big fleet orders.
- **Best sellers:** the Ford Transit Custom (8,900), Mercedes-Benz Sprinter (3,166) and Ford Transit (2,969). These counts cover all fuel types, not just electric.

SMMT's electric total includes heavier electric vans of 3.5 to 4.25 tonnes, which count towards makers' targets. These rose from 260 to 654. Electric vans up to 3.5 tonnes grew by 4.4% to 4,178, a little slower than the market overall.

So far this year, 27,359 electric vans have been registered, up 23.7%. After August, when electric vans took a record 16.3% of a quiet month, the year-to-date share was 11.0%. September's lower share pulled it back to 10.8%.

## Why it's behind

There's plenty of choice: SMMT says more than 40 electric van models are on sale, and Mike Hawes says makers are offering substantial discounts. But SMMT names higher purchase costs, depot upgrades, grid delays and a lack of charging suited to vans as the main barriers.

SMMT doesn't expect the gap to close soon. Its July outlook forecasts electric vans up to 3.5 tonnes taking 11.5% of the market this year, rising to 15.9% in 2027. SMMT says that leaves take-up around two years behind the mandate. A revised outlook is due by 5 November.
