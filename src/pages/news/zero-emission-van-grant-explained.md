Since April 2026, the government's discount on new electric vans and trucks has been called the **Zero Emission Van Grant** and **Zero Emission Truck Grant**. They are an updated version of the old Plug-in Van Grant and Plug-in Truck Grant, paid by the Office for Zero Emission Vehicles.

According to a report by the Competition and Markets Authority's Subsidy Advice Unit, published on 24 March 2026, the scheme is planned to run from 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2030, or until its **£877 million** budget runs out. The report, which is non-binding advice to the Office for Zero Emission Vehicles, gives grant levels only for the 2026/2027 financial year. The grants were announced on 25 March 2026 as part of a **£1 billion** package for electric vans, trucks and depot chargers.

## Vans

| Van | Gross vehicle weight | Grant |
|---|---|---|
| Small | Under 2,500kg | Up to £2,500 |
| Large | 2,500kg to 4,250kg | Up to £5,000 |

To qualify, a van must produce zero CO2 at the tailpipe, travel at least 60 miles (96km) without any emissions, and be on the government's approved list. GOV.UK's vans page also says: "Some vans can be sold at a 35% discount." It doesn't explain this further, so ask the dealer what applies. Browse models on our [van pages](/vans/).

Small vans on the list include the Citroën e-Berlingo and Vauxhall Combo-e. Large ones include the Renault Trafic E-Tech, Kia PV5 Cargo and some versions of the Ford E-Transit Custom. New versions of an approved van aren't automatically covered, so check the exact model and trim.

## Trucks

In England, Scotland and Wales, the grant is a cash cap or a share of the upfront cost:

| Truck weight | Most you can get | Old Plug-in Truck Grant cap |
|---|---|---|
| 4.25 to 12 tonnes | £15,000 or 20%, whichever is lower | £20,000 |
| 12 to 18 tonnes | £37,000 or 40%, whichever is lower | £60,000 |
| 18 to 26 tonnes | £52,000 or 40%, whichever is lower | £80,000 |
| Over 26 tonnes | £81,000 or 40%, whichever is lower | £120,000 |

Old caps are from the Subsidy Advice Unit's report. In Northern Ireland, small and medium-sized firms get the same rates, but large firms are capped at 30% for trucks over 12 tonnes.

## How you get it

- **The dealer does it.** The dealer or manufacturer takes it off the price at the point of sale.
- **Buying or leasing** both count.
- **New only.** The grant is for new vans and trucks at first registration. Used vehicles don't qualify. Conversions done after registration don't count.
- **Timing matters.** The dealer must put your order on the government's grant portal before the vehicle is registered, and not on the same day.
- **Your rate is locked in.** The grant is locked in at the level advertised when the dealer puts your order on the portal, as long as the vehicle is registered and delivered within 12 months. GOV.UK says this 12-month window is a temporary extension that the Office for Zero Emission Vehicles can change at any time. A grant isn't guaranteed until the vehicle has been validated through the portal.
- **What "price" means.** The grant comes off the purchase price after any discount. For this, the price includes VAT (where it applies), number plates and road tax, but not optional extras, delivery charges or the first registration fee. Ask for an invoice with the grant shown separately.

## Limits and catches

- **Annual cap.** Each business, organisation or individual gets up to 1,500 van grants and 100 truck grants each financial year (1 April to 31 March).
- **Leased vehicles count against you,** the end customer, not the leasing company.
- **Linked companies** in the same group can be treated as one business.
- **Orders over the limit are cancelled,** and the government can claw the money back if a customer goes over.
- **UK only.** The vehicle must be registered in the UK, with UK VAT paid.
- **Grant levels and rules can change** at the government's discretion, and the money is limited.

## Other help

- **Depot chargers.** The same package gave the Depot Charging Scheme £170 million. As the government said in March 2026, businesses can get up to 70% of the cost, up to £1 million, for chargers for vans, coaches and electric HGVs. See our [depot charging guide](/guides/depot-charging/).
- **Workplace chargers.** The Workplace Charging Scheme pays up to £500 per socket, for up to 40 sockets across your sites, until 31 March 2027.
