Two UK van titles have road tested the PV5 Cargo. Parkers scored it 4.5 out of 5 and Auto Express also rated it 4.5. Parkers named it its 2026 Van of the Year, while Auto Express made it its Electric Van of the Year and Small Van of the Year for 2026.

### What they tested

Parkers' Tom Webster drove the van carrying a 400kg load. He doesn't say which battery or body, but Auto Express says the L2H1 was the only body on sale at the time. Auto Express's Shane Wilkinson tested the Standard Range 51.5kWh van around East Sussex with 350kg of sand in the back. Both reviews were published in December 2025, so neither covers the shorter L1H1 or taller L2H2 bodies.

### Driving

Parkers found it drives and rides very well, with steering that feels sharp in town but weights up nicely at speed, and judged it very refined and quiet. Auto Express's tester reckoned it feels like a big car from behind the wheel, and said its test van felt quicker than its official acceleration figure suggests.

### Range in the real world

Auto Express averaged 3.1 miles per kWh from the Standard Range battery in town and on rural roads at about 11 degrees. It worked that out as roughly 159 miles of real-world range, against the 184-mile official figure it quotes. Parkers' early impression is that Kia's range readouts are fairly accurate. The Long Range 71.2kWh battery is rated at 258 miles, and Auto Express says a 10 to 80 per cent rapid charge at up to 150kW should take less than half an hour. See our [charging guide](/guides/charging-an-electric-van/).

One glitch: Auto Express's test van wouldn't allow stronger regenerative braking than level one until the battery dropped below 85 per cent. Kia told the magazine a software update would fix it.

### Load area and practicality

Both testers like the very low loading lip, which Parkers lists at 419mm at the rear and 399mm at the side door. The catch, Parkers explains, is that the floor sits below the top of the rear suspension, creating extra intrusions at the rear corners. It says a Euro pallet fits through the back sideways "but only just", and that Kia offers a separate flat floor if you need a level load bay. Both say the side door is too narrow for a Euro pallet. Auto Express also found the rear doors needed a very firm slam before the sensors accepted they were shut.

Auto Express quotes 4.4 cubic metres of space in the L2H1, enough for two Euro pallets, and a maximum payload of 690kg with the Long Range battery and 790kg with the Standard Range. Parkers still reckons the bigger battery is worth the 100kg payload penalty. In a June 2025 news story Parkers reported that payload would rise to 805kg in the shorter L1H1, but in July 2026 Auto Express said Kia still offered only one body, so we haven't used that figure.

### Cabin and tech

Parkers liked the smart cabin and its hard-wearing materials, but found storage mixed and complained that the centre armrest sits lower than the one on the driver's door. Auto Express called it well built but heavy on budget plastics, and praised seats that stayed comfortable over many hours. Both dislike having the climate controls on the touchscreen, and Auto Express found the driver attention warning nagged whenever it glanced at the screen. A heat pump is an option on the Plus trim only.

### Price: reports differ

Parkers' best electric vans guide (updated 18 November 2025) lists the PV5 from £27,645, excluding VAT and before the Plug-in Van Grant. Auto Express's best electric vans list (27 July 2026) puts it from £27,355 excluding VAT, but after the grant. The two figures are on different bases and don't line up, and we couldn't check Kia's own price list, so get a written quote. Our [electric vs diesel calculator](/tools/electric-vs-diesel/) compares running costs. Both testers rate its value and its seven-year, 100,000-mile warranty highly. In the same June 2025 story, Parkers reported an eight-year battery warranty, with Kia guaranteeing at least 70% of the battery's original performance.

### Where testers disagree

Visibility splits opinion even within Auto Express. Its road tester found side and rear visibility poor, with a huge blind spot behind, while its awards write-up said the large windows and mirrors give good visibility all round. Noise divides the two magazines: Parkers found it hushed, but Auto Express noted wind noise and tyre roar as speed rises. More on our [Kia PV5 Cargo van page](/vans/kia-pv5-cargo/).
