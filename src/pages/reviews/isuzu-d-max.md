Isuzu has done something its electric rivals haven't: built a battery pickup that matches the diesel D-Max where pickup buyers look first. It carries over a tonne, tows 3.5 tonnes and has permanent four-wheel drive. Everything else is a trade-off.

### Who it's for

Estates, farms, utility crews and councils whose trucks work hard but don't go far, and who want a zero-emission vehicle that can still pull a full trailer. There are two trims, eDL40 and eV-Cross, in Extended Cab and Double Cab bodies. There's no single cab, and no two-seat version with the rear seats blanked off.

### Load bed, payload and towing

Payload is 1,060kg for the Extended Cab and 1,015kg for the Double Cab, thanks to new rear suspension in place of the diesel's leaf springs. Braked towing is 3,500kg, same as the diesel. The bed is 1,785mm long in the Extended Cab and 1,520mm in the Double Cab, and 1,530mm wide and 500mm deep in both. The trade-off for that extra length is that the Extended Cab's rear seats are only fit for short trips.

### Tax: does it count as a commercial vehicle?

For VAT, yes. Under HMRC rules a double cab with a payload of one tonne or more isn't treated as a car for VAT, so a VAT-registered business can usually reclaim it. Company car tax is different: since 6 April 2025 HMRC expects most double cabs, extended cabs included, to be treated as cars for benefit in kind. Zero emissions keep that bill low, though. See our [grants and tax guide](/guides/grants-and-tax/).

### Range and charging in the real world

The 66.9kWh battery gives a 163-mile official range. Isuzu says 224 miles in city driving is more representative of slow farm and estate work. On an hour-long drive on UK roads, one test saw 2.5 miles per kWh, which works out at about 167 miles.

Charging is the weak spot. The fastest DC rate is 50kW, so 20 to 80 per cent takes about an hour. Overnight on 11kW three-phase AC, a full charge takes 10 hours. Plan for depot charging; our [charging guide](/guides/charging-an-electric-van/) explains the options.

### On the road

By all accounts the D-Max EV has a reputation for being very quiet and much keener than the diesel once moving, with 0-62mph in 10.1 seconds. Reports on the ride are less kind: firm and a bit bouncy with an empty bed. Neither test carried a load or went properly off road, so we can't say how it copes on rough ground. The specs are encouraging: permanent four-wheel drive, the standard Rough Terrain Mode, and 210mm of ground clearance and a 600mm wading depth, both 20mm less than the diesel.

### Cab and kit

Both trims come with leather upholstery, a powered driver's seat and heated front seats, plus real buttons and dials for basics such as volume. The cab is otherwise the same as the diesel's, so there's no front boot.

### Warranty and servicing

The truck has Isuzu's five-year, 125,000-mile warranty, but the battery is covered for eight years but only 100,000 miles. Servicing is every 12,000 miles or 12 months.

### Alternatives

The [Toyota Hilux electric](/vans/toyota-hilux/), [KGM Musso EV](/vans/kgm-musso/) and [Maxus eTerron 9](/vans/maxus-eterron-9/) all fall short of a tonne, so they count as cars for VAT. The Musso and eTerron go further on a charge. If you need more range, a diesel [D-Max](/vans/isuzu-d-max/) or a [Ford Ranger](/vans/ford-ranger/) plug-in hybrid may suit better.

### Price

In May 2026 the D-Max EV was reported from £59,995 excluding VAT, and the Extended Cab eDL40 is the cheapest version. That report didn't say whether the grant was included, so we haven't scored price. Earlier reports had the Double Cab about £1,000 more and the eV-Cross Double Cab £62,495, both ex VAT, in April 2026: nearly £20,000 more than an equivalent diesel automatic. The D-Max EV is on the government's approved list for the Plug-in Van Grant, worth up to £5,000 on some large vans.
