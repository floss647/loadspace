Isuzu has done something its electric rivals haven't: built a battery pickup that matches the diesel D-Max where pickup buyers look first. It carries over a tonne, tows 3.5 tonnes and has permanent four-wheel drive. Everything else is a trade-off.

### Who it's for

Estates, farms, utility crews and councils whose trucks work hard, don't go far and still need to pull a full trailer. There are two trims, eDL40 and eV-Cross, in Extended Cab and Double Cab bodies. There's no single cab, and no two-seat version with the rear seats blanked off.

### Load bed, payload and towing

Payload is 1,060kg for the Extended Cab and 1,015kg for the Double Cab, thanks to new rear suspension in place of the diesel's leaf springs. Braked towing is 3,500kg, same as the diesel. Isuzu gives the Extended Cab's bed as 1,805mm long; the Double Cab's has been measured at 1,520mm, and both are 1,530mm wide and 500mm deep. The catch: the Extended Cab's rear seats are only fit for short trips.

### Tax: does it count as a commercial vehicle?

Check this with your accountant. HMRC's VAT manual says a vehicle with a payload of one tonne or more isn't treated as a car, payload being the maximum gross weight minus the kerb weight. Both bodies clear that as built, at 1,060kg and 1,015kg. HMRC also warns that adding accessories can lower the payload enough to turn a double cab into a car, most likely on one with an ex-works payload of 1,000 to 1,050kg, which takes in the Double Cab. Under a 1999 agreement with the motor industry, a hard top counts as 45kg.

For company car tax, from 6 April 2025 HMRC stopped using the VAT payload test for benefit in kind. It judges each double cab as a whole, extended cabs included, on whether its construction is primarily suited to carrying goods, and expects most to be classed as cars. Transitional rules cover only pickups bought, leased or ordered before 6 April 2025, until the earlier of disposal, lease expiry or 5 April 2029. See our [grants and tax guide](/guides/grants-and-tax/).

### Range and charging in the real world

Isuzu quotes a WLTP range of 263km (about 163 miles) from the 66.9kWh battery. On an hour-long drive on UK roads, one test's trip computer showed 2.5 miles per kWh, or about 167 miles.

Charging is the weak spot. Isuzu gives the fastest DC rate as 50kW, with 20 to 80 per cent in about an hour. On 11kW AC, Isuzu quotes 10 hours from empty to full. Our [charging guide](/guides/charging-an-electric-van/) covers depot options.

### On the road

Across the two road tests we've seen, the D-Max EV has a reputation for being very quiet and more responsive than the diesel once moving. Reports on the ride differ: one found it well judged though still on the bouncy side, the other called it firm and bumpy with an empty bed. Neither published test went off road. On paper it has permanent four-wheel drive, the standard Rough Terrain Mode, and 210mm of ground clearance and a 600mm wading depth, both 20mm less than the diesel.

### Cab and kit

Both trims come with leather upholstery, a powered driver's seat and heated front seats, plus real buttons and dials for basics such as volume. But there's no front boot.

### Warranty

The truck has Isuzu's five-year, 125,000-mile warranty, while Isuzu's UK site gives the battery warranty as 8 years or 100,000 miles.

### Alternatives

None of its electric rivals matches both its payload and towing on published figures: the [Toyota Hilux electric](/vans/toyota-hilux/) is rated at 715kg and the [KGM Musso EV](/vans/kgm-musso/) is also under a tonne. The Musso and the [Maxus eTerron 9](/vans/maxus-eterron-9/) have longer claimed ranges, though. If you need more range, a diesel [D-Max](/vans/isuzu-d-max/) or a [Ford Ranger](/vans/ford-ranger/) plug-in hybrid may suit better.

### Price

Isuzu's UK site lists the Extended Cab eDL40, the cheapest version, from £54,995 plus VAT on the road, and says that price includes the van grant. The large-van grant is a 35% discount up to a maximum of £5,000, which on this price means the full £5,000, so before the grant the Extended Cab eDL40 is £59,995 ex VAT. The eDL40 Double Cab is £1,000 more, and the eV-Cross Double Cab is listed from £57,495 plus VAT with the grant, or £62,495 before it. An April 2026 report put the EV at nearly £20,000 more than an equivalent diesel automatic, ex VAT. The D-Max EV is on the government's approved list for the Plug-in Van Grant.
