### Who it's for

If you run a diesel Transit Custom, or were about to order one, this is the electric van to look at first. In the ways that matter on the job it's the same van: the same load volume, the same cab and load bay layout, and the same racking fits in the back. It makes most sense for trades and fleets on predictable daily routes who can charge at base overnight. It only comes with a low roof, under 2 metres tall, so look elsewhere if you need standing room in the back. The upside is fewer problems with height-restricted car parks.

### Load area and payload

Space is the big draw. You choose a short or long wheelbase, and neither gives anything away to the diesel in load volume.

Payload is where the battery costs you. On Ford's price list, effective from 1 June 2026, the best panel van is the long-wheelbase Limited with rear-wheel drive at 1,126 kg, with the long-wheelbase Trend just 2kg behind. That's respectable, but payloads are lower than the diesel's, and plenty of versions carry under 1,000kg. All-wheel drive is the big hit: on Ford's figures it costs a Trend about 170kg of payload. If you load to the limit, check the plated figure for your exact van before you add racking. Towing is a strength, though: every version is rated to pull a 2,300kg braked trailer.

### Range and charging in the real world

Ford quotes an official WLTP range of up to 230 miles. That figure is for the short-wheelbase Trend with the 136PS motor and rear-wheel drive, which comes with a 70mph speed limiter. Ford's own WLTP motorway figure for the same van is 172 miles, a more useful guide if you spend your day on the motorway. All-wheel drive cuts the official figure to 212 miles, and the short-wheelbase MS-RT with rear-wheel drive is rated at 173 miles. Add a full load and a cold morning and you'll get less again, so plan winter work around well under the headline figure. Every version has a heat pump as standard, which helps in the cold.

Rapid charging runs at up to 125kW. Ford gives two different times for a 10 to 80% rapid charge: 29 minutes in its price list and about 39 minutes on its website, so plan around the slower one. By Ford's figures, 10 minutes on a rapid charger adds 69 miles. Back at base, 10 to 80% takes 4 hours 40 minutes on an 11kW three-phase charger, or 6 hours 42 minutes on a 7.4kW single-phase one, so an overnight top-up is easy. Our [charging guide](/guides/charging-an-electric-van/) covers the rest.

### On the road

We haven't driven the E-Transit Custom ourselves, but its reputation is consistent. It has a reputation as one of the most car-like vans to drive, with a comfortable ride even over broken town roads, and it's said to be quiet at speed, with wind and tyre noise well kept down.

There are three motors: 136PS, 218PS and 285PS (the last only in the MS-RT), with rear- or all-wheel drive. By all accounts even the 136PS version has plenty in reserve for motorway work, and that's the one we'd pick. The MS-RT is generally seen as fun but hard to justify.

### Cab and kit

The gear selector is a stalk on the steering column and the parking brake switch sits between the centre air vents, so the floor is flat and it's easy to move across the cab. Every version gets a 13-inch touchscreen with Apple CarPlay and Android Auto. Our gripe is that the climate controls are on that screen, which is fiddlier than proper knobs, although there are shortcuts at the bottom of the screen. The optional Mobile Office pack adds a steering wheel that tilts to become a tablet stand or lunch tray. Even the Trend has adaptive cruise control, a reversing camera and front and rear parking sensors as standard.

### Which version to buy

Buy the Trend with the 136PS motor and rear-wheel drive. In short-wheelbase form it has the longest official range, and the standard kit is generous enough that you don't need to go higher. Choose the long wheelbase if you need the length: it costs £700 more. Limited adds heated seats and a hands-free power sliding door for £1,550 over the equivalent Trend, which is worth it only if you'll use the door every day. All-wheel drive adds £5,000 to a Trend and costs you payload and range, so only pick it if you really need the traction. We'd skip the MS-RT.

### Alternatives

The [Volkswagen e-Transporter](/vans/volkswagen-transporter/) shares its underpinnings with the Ford through a partnership between the two companies, so get a quote for both. Otherwise, compare it with the [Vauxhall Vivaro Electric](/vans/vauxhall-vivaro/), [Toyota Proace Electric](/vans/toyota-proace/), [Kia PV5 Cargo](/vans/kia-pv5-cargo/) and [Volkswagen ID. Buzz Cargo](/vans/volkswagen-id-buzz-cargo/). Some rivals go further on a charge, so check their figures if range matters more to you than payload.

### Price

Ford lists the E-Transit Custom from £43,630 before VAT, or £45,509.83 on the road before VAT, on its price list effective from 1 June 2026. That climbs to £66,190 for a long-wheelbase MS-RT with all-wheel drive. It's not cheap, and there's no basic trim to bring the entry price down. Prices change, so get a written quote, and read our [grants and tax guide](/guides/grants-and-tax/) before you order. The battery is covered for 8 years or 100,000 miles, and the van for 3 years or 100,000 miles. Servicing is every 2 years with no mileage limit.
