verdict: "This is the electric medium van we'd look at first. It gives up nothing to the diesel in load space, every version has a heat pump, and by all accounts it drives much like a car. The catches are payloads below the diesel's and a starting price of £43,160 before VAT and before the government van grant, with no basic trim to bring that down. Buy the 136PS Trend with rear-wheel drive unless your work really needs more."

### Who it's for

If you run a diesel Transit Custom, or were about to order one, start here. On the job it's the same van: the same load volume, the same cab and load bay layout, and the same racking fits in the back. It suits trades and fleets on predictable routes who can charge at base overnight. It only comes with a low roof, under 2 metres tall, so no standing room, but fewer problems with height-restricted car parks.

### Load area and payload

Space is the big draw: whether you choose a short or long wheelbase, neither gives anything away to the diesel in load volume.

Payload is where the battery costs you. On Ford's price list, effective from 11 September 2026, the best panel van is the long-wheelbase Trend with rear-wheel drive at 1,132kg, with the long-wheelbase Limited just 8kg behind. But payloads are lower than the diesel's, plenty of versions carry under 1,000kg, and on Ford's figures all-wheel drive costs a Trend about 170kg. Check the plated figure for your exact van before you add racking. Towing is a strength: every panel van is rated to pull a 2,300kg braked trailer, though some double cab-in-van and Multicab versions are rated lower, at 2,250kg or 2,000kg.

### Range and charging in the real world

Ford's price list (effective 11 September 2026) gives an official range of up to 237 miles, for the short-wheelbase Trend with the 136PS motor and rear-wheel drive. Its motorway figure for the same van is 180 miles, a better guide for motorway work. All-wheel drive cuts the official figure to 213 miles. Ford's UK website still shows the earlier £43,630 price and 230-mile range. A full load and a cold morning will cut that further, so plan winter work well under the headline figure. Every version has a heat pump as standard, which helps.

Rapid charging runs at up to 125kW. Ford gives two times for a 10 to 80% rapid charge, 29 minutes in its price list and about 39 minutes on its website, so plan around the slower one. Back at base, 10 to 80% takes 4 hours 40 minutes on an 11kW three-phase charger, or 6 hours 42 minutes on a 7.4kW single-phase one, so overnight charging is easy. Our [charging guide](/guides/charging-an-electric-van/) covers the rest.

### On the road

We haven't driven it ourselves, but it has a reputation for being car-like to drive, with a ride that copes well with bumpy town roads. It's also said to be quiet once under way, though the motor can whine noticeably as you pull away and come to a stop.

There are three motors, 136PS, 218PS and 285PS (the last only in the MS-RT on Ford's current price list), with rear- or all-wheel drive. By all accounts even the 136PS version has enough punch for motorway work, and that's the one we'd pick. The MS-RT is quicker still, but it's generally seen as hard to justify.

### Cab and kit

With the gear selector on a steering column stalk and the parking brake switch between the centre air vents, the floor is flat and it's easy to move across the cab. Every version gets a 13-inch touchscreen with Apple CarPlay and Android Auto, but the climate controls are on that screen too, which is fiddlier than proper knobs. The optional Mobile Office pack adds a steering wheel that tilts to become a tablet stand or lunch tray. Even the Trend has cruise control, a reversing camera and front and rear parking sensors as standard, though adaptive cruise is part of an optional camera pack.

### Which version to buy

Buy the Trend with the 136PS motor and rear-wheel drive. In short-wheelbase form it has the longest official range, and the standard kit is generous enough that you don't need to go higher. The long wheelbase costs £700 more. Limited adds heated front seats and keyless entry for £1,550 over the equivalent Trend. A hands-free power sliding door is an extra-cost option on every trim. All-wheel drive adds £5,000 to a Trend and costs payload and range, so only pick it if you need the traction. We'd skip the MS-RT.

### Alternatives

The [Volkswagen e-Transporter](/vans/volkswagen-transporter/) shares its underpinnings with the Ford through a partnership between the two companies, so get a quote for both. Otherwise, compare it with the [Vauxhall Vivaro Electric](/vans/vauxhall-vivaro/), [Toyota Proace Electric](/vans/toyota-proace/), [Kia PV5 Cargo](/vans/kia-pv5-cargo/) and [Volkswagen ID. Buzz Cargo](/vans/volkswagen-id-buzz-cargo/). Some rivals go further on a charge.

### Price

Ford lists it from £43,160 before VAT, or £45,039.83 on the road before VAT, on its price list effective from 11 September 2026. Both figures are before the government's Plug-in Van Grant, which Ford shows as a separate £5,000 saving. For the panel van, the list price before VAT rises to £65,720 for a long-wheelbase MS-RT with all-wheel drive. Prices change, so get a written quote, and read our [grants and tax guide](/guides/grants-and-tax/) before you order. The battery is covered for 8 years or 100,000 miles and the van for 3 years or 100,000 miles, with servicing every 2 years and no mileage limit. To compare running costs with a diesel, try our [electric vs diesel calculator](/tools/electric-vs-diesel/).
