The electric Hilux is Toyota's answer to a narrow question: what does a fleet need when it must go off road, can't burn diesel and doesn't travel far? Airside work, forestry and highway maintenance are the jobs it is aimed at, and the spec sheet shows it.

### Who it's for

Think depot-based crews on short, predictable shifts who still need four-wheel drive. It only comes as a double cab, in Icon and Invincible trims. If your pickup spends its week towing a digger or hauling a tonne of aggregate, this isn't the one.

### Load area, payload and towing

Because the battery sits inside the chassis rails, the bed is the same size as the diesel's: 1,555mm long, 1,540mm wide and 480mm deep. What you can put in it is the problem. The double cab's payload is 715 kg, and it tows 1,600kg braked, where the diesel can tow 3.5 tonnes.

In August 2026 a factory two-seat conversion went on sale. It replaces the rear bench with a flat, secure load area of 1,290mm wide and 640mm deep, but we haven't seen a published payload for it, so we haven't scored payload.

### Range and charging in the real world

The 59.2kWh battery is small for a vehicle this heavy, and the official range is quoted differently. Toyota's launch figure is up to 149 miles, and the most recent report we've seen, from July 2026, repeats it, so that's the figure we've scored; a June 2026 first drive quotes Toyota as giving about 160 miles WLTP. We haven't found a measured UK figure yet.

Toyota quotes rapid charging at up to 150kW, good for 10 to 80 per cent in less than half an hour, and a 10kW wallbox charges it fully in about six and a half hours. Our [charging guide](/guides/charging-an-electric-van/) covers depot set-ups.

### On the road and off it

Across the early tests, the electric Hilux has a reputation for being far quieter and more comfortable on the road than the diesel, though wind noise becomes more obvious at motorway speeds and the steering needs a lot of turns at low speed. Bear in mind that the earliest, from November 2025, was not on UK roads.

Off road it keeps the 29-degree approach angle and 700mm wading depth, though the breakover angle is shallower. There are no diff locks or low-range gears; instead there's a terrain system with rock, sand, mud, dirt and mogul modes.

### Cab and kit

The cab gets two 12.3-inch screens, and Toyota has kept physical climate controls, which you'll appreciate in gloves. The battery cover is service-activated and can run for up to 10 years or 1,000,000 km, alongside the 10-year, 100,000-mile service-activated warranty.

### Tax: van or car?

This matters more than usual, so check with your accountant before you order. HMRC's VAT manual says a vehicle with a payload of one tonne or more isn't treated as a car, payload being the maximum gross weight minus the kerb weight. The double cab's 715 kg is well short of that.

Company car tax works differently: from 6 April 2025 HMRC stopped using that VAT payload test for benefit in kind. It now looks at each double cab as a whole, judging whether its construction is primarily suited to carrying goods, and it expects most double cabs to be classed as cars. Transitional rules cover only pickups bought, leased or ordered before 6 April 2025, until the earlier of disposal, lease expiry or 5 April 2029. The two-seat conversion is aimed at this: it has been reported as qualifying for van tax treatment. More in our [grants and tax guide](/guides/grants-and-tax/).

### Alternatives

The [Isuzu D-Max EV](/vans/isuzu-d-max/) carries over a tonne and tows 3.5 tonnes. The [KGM Musso EV](/vans/kgm-musso/) and [Maxus eTerron 9](/vans/maxus-eterron-9/) have longer claimed ranges, and if range is the sticking point, a [Ford Ranger](/vans/ford-ranger/) plug-in hybrid is the stop-gap.

### Price

In May 2026, Toyota's UK prices were reported as £57,845 for the Icon and £60,695 for the Invincible, both before the £5,000 Plug-in Van Grant, and order books opened on 1 June. The same report gives the diesel's starting price including VAT but doesn't say whether the electric prices do, so we haven't scored price. Both electric trims are on the government's approved list for the Plug-in Van Grant. The two-seat conversion is listed from £49,356 excluding VAT on the road, after a £5,000 grant discount, which makes £54,356 before it.
