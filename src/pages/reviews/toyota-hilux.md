The electric Hilux is Toyota's answer to a narrow question: what does a fleet need when it must go off road, can't burn diesel and doesn't travel far? Airside work, forestry and site maintenance are the jobs it was built for, and the spec sheet shows it.

### Who it's for

Think depot-based crews on short, predictable shifts who still need four-wheel drive. It only comes as a double cab, in Icon and Invincible trims. If your pickup spends its week towing a digger or hauling a tonne of aggregate, this isn't the one.

### Load area, payload and towing

Because the battery sits inside the chassis rails, the bed is the same size as the diesel's: 1,555mm long, 1,540mm wide and 480mm deep. What you can put in it is the problem. Payload is 715 kg, and it tows 1,600kg braked, where the diesel can tow 3.5 tonnes.

In August 2026 Toyota added a factory two-seat conversion that replaces the rear bench with a flat, lockable load area of 1,290mm wide and 640mm deep. Toyota says it qualifies under van tax rules. Its payload hasn't been published.

### Range and charging in the real world

The 59.2kWh battery is small for a vehicle this heavy, and reports differ on the official figure: one launch report gives up to 149 miles, while a June 2026 first drive quotes Toyota's figure as about 160 miles WLTP. We haven't found a measured UK figure yet.

Rapid charging runs at up to 150kW, good for 10 to 80 per cent in less than half an hour, and a 10kW wallbox charges it fully in about six and a half hours. Our [charging guide](/guides/charging-an-electric-van/) covers depot set-ups.

### On the road and off it

The electric Hilux has a reputation for being far quieter and more comfortable on the road than the diesel, though wind noise becomes more obvious at motorway speeds and the steering needs a lot of turns at low speed. Note that the earliest test drove a pre-production truck abroad.

Off road it keeps the 29-degree approach angle and 700mm wading depth, though the breakover angle is shallower. There are no diff locks or low-range gears; instead there's a terrain system with rock, sand, mud, dirt and mogul modes.

### Cab and kit

The cab gets two 12.3-inch screens, and Toyota has kept physical climate controls, which you'll appreciate in gloves. The battery is covered for up to 10 years or 1,000,000 km as long as the truck is serviced by Toyota, alongside the 10-year, 100,000-mile service-activated warranty.

### Tax: van or car?

This matters more than usual. HMRC only stops treating a double cab as a car for VAT when its payload is one tonne or more, and since 6 April 2025 HMRC expects most double cabs to be taxed as cars for company car tax. With 715 kg, the standard electric Hilux is a car for VAT. Zero emissions keep company car tax low, though. More in our [grants and tax guide](/guides/grants-and-tax/).

### Alternatives

The [Isuzu D-Max EV](/vans/isuzu-d-max/) carries over a tonne and tows 3.5 tonnes. The [KGM Musso EV](/vans/kgm-musso/) and [Maxus eTerron 9](/vans/maxus-eterron-9/) go further on a charge, and a [Ford Ranger](/vans/ford-ranger/) plug-in hybrid avoids range worries altogether.

### Price

In May 2026, Toyota's UK prices were reported as £57,845 for the Icon and £60,695 for the Invincible, both before the £5,000 Plug-in Van Grant, and order books opened on 1 June. Reports don't say whether those figures include VAT, so we haven't scored price. Both electric trims are on the government's approved list for the Plug-in Van Grant. The two-seat conversion is listed from £49,356 excluding VAT, after a £5,000 grant discount.
