The electric Hilux is Toyota's answer to a narrow question: what does a fleet need when it must go off road, can't burn diesel and doesn't travel far? Airside work, forestry and highway maintenance are the jobs it is aimed at, and the spec sheet shows it.

### Who it's for

Think depot-based crews on short, predictable shifts who still need four-wheel drive. It only comes as a double cab, in Icon and Invincible trims. If your pickup spends its week towing a digger or hauling a tonne of aggregate, this isn't the one.

### Load area, payload and towing

Because the battery sits inside the chassis rails, the bed is the same size as the diesel's: 1,555mm long, 1,540mm wide and 480mm deep. What you can put in it is the problem. Toyota gives the electric Hilux a maximum payload of up to 715kg. For towing, Toyota's headline maximum is 1,600kg, the braked figure early reports also give, where the diesel can tow 3.5 tonnes; but the spec panel and FAQ on the same Toyota page say up to 1,700kg, so check the plate.

In August 2026 a factory two-seat conversion went on sale. It replaces the rear bench with a flat, secure load area of 1,290mm wide and 640mm deep. Toyota doesn't publish a separate payload for it, so we've scored the 715 kg maximum.

### Range and charging in the real world

The 59.2kWh battery is small for a vehicle this heavy. Toyota's UK site now gives a WLTP range of up to 159 miles, which is the figure we've scored; early reports quoted 149 miles at launch. We haven't found a measured UK figure yet.

Toyota quotes rapid charging at up to 150kW, good for 10 to 80 per cent in about 30 minutes, and a 10kW wallbox charges it fully in about six and a half hours, by one early report. Our [charging guide](/guides/charging-an-electric-van/) covers depot set-ups.

### On the road and off it

Across the early tests, the electric Hilux has a reputation for being far quieter and more comfortable on the road than the diesel, though wind noise becomes more obvious at motorway speeds and the steering needs a lot of turns at low speed. Bear in mind that the earliest, from November 2025, was not on UK roads.

Off road it keeps the 29-degree approach angle and 700mm wading depth, though the breakover angle is shallower. There are no diff locks or low-range gears; instead there's a terrain system with rock, sand, mud, dirt and mogul modes.

### Cab and kit

The cab gets two 12.3-inch screens, and Toyota has kept physical climate controls, which you'll appreciate in gloves. The battery cover is service-activated: each qualifying Toyota service renews Toyota's Battery Care warranty until the pickup is 10 years old or has covered 1 million miles, alongside the 10-year, 100,000-mile service-activated warranty on the rest of the vehicle.

### Tax: van or car?

This matters more than usual, so check with your accountant before you order. HMRC's VAT manual says a vehicle with a payload of one tonne or more isn't treated as a car, payload being the maximum gross weight minus the kerb weight. The double cab's 715 kg is well short of that, and Toyota itself says the electric Hilux is treated as a car for VAT purposes.

Company car tax works differently: from 6 April 2025 HMRC stopped using that VAT payload test for benefit in kind. It now looks at each double cab as a whole, judging whether its construction is primarily suited to carrying goods, and it expects most double cabs to be classed as cars. Transitional rules cover only pickups bought, leased or ordered before 6 April 2025, until the earlier of disposal, lease expiry or 5 April 2029. The two-seat conversion is aimed at this: Toyota says it enables flat-rate benefit-in-kind taxation and a 100 per cent plant and machinery allowance, where applicable. More in our [grants and tax guide](/guides/grants-and-tax/).

### Alternatives

The [Isuzu D-Max EV](/vans/isuzu-d-max/) carries over a tonne and tows 3.5 tonnes. The [KGM Musso EV](/vans/kgm-musso/) and [Maxus eTerron 9](/vans/maxus-eterron-9/) have longer claimed ranges, and if range is the sticking point, a [Ford Ranger](/vans/ford-ranger/) plug-in hybrid is the stop-gap.

### Price

Toyota's UK price list, effective from 1 October 2026, starts the electric Hilux at £47,170 excluding VAT and before the grant, for the Icon double cab; the Invincible is £49,545. On the road with VAT, Toyota gives £57,845 for the Icon and £60,695 for the Invincible, both before the £5,000 Plug-in Van Grant. Both electric trims are on the government's approved list for the Plug-in Van Grant. The two-seat Icon is listed at £48,253 excluding VAT and before the grant. Toyota's own figures disagree on whether its quoted on-the-road price for the conversion includes the grant, so ask for a written breakdown.
