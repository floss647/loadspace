### Who it's for

The e-Transporter is Volkswagen's electric medium van, developed jointly with Ford alongside the [Ford E-Transit Custom](/vans/ford-transit-custom/) and reported to be built on the same production line as the Ford. One review says it launched with the same motor and battery, though another reports a slightly smaller battery than the Ford's. Read our [E-Transit Custom review](/reviews/ford-transit-custom/) too. It suits local or regional work with somewhere to charge overnight.

### Load area and payload

There are two lengths, both with a roof comfortably under 2 metres, and the battery costs nothing in load space compared with the diesel: Volkswagen quotes 5.8 cubic metres in the short van and 6.8 in the long one. The long van takes three Euro pallets, and an 8ft by 4ft board fits between the wheel arches.

Volkswagen's website puts electric panel van payloads at roughly 911kg to 1,081kg including a driver, depending on specification. Its brochure gives a best of 1,156kg for the long-wheelbase 136hp van, but that's worked out as gross weight minus kerb weight with no driver aboard, so check the plated figure for your exact van. Every version can tow a 2,300kg trailer.

### Range and charging in the real world

Volkswagen's current UK price list offers a 70kWh battery for every trim, though a few older 65kWh Commerce Pro S vans are listed as stock. VW quotes up to 225 miles on the official WLTP test for the short-wheelbase 136hp Commerce Plus, and 210 miles for the long-wheelbase 218hp Commerce Pro. Launch vans had a smaller battery and were reported at up to 201 miles. Reports of the bigger battery in January 2026 gave up to 237 miles, but VW's UK site quotes 225.

In one week-long test of a launch van, the dash showed 160 miles to empty at 99% charge, and that looked about right over a week of light loads. VW quotes a 10 to 80% rapid charge in about 30 minutes at up to 125kW, and its brochure gives 10.5 hours for a full charge on a 7.4kW wallbox. Our [charging guide](/guides/charging-an-electric-van/) covers the rest.

### On the road

We haven't driven it. One review found it easy to handle in town, with light steering and a ride that soaks up bumps, and others describe it as quiet and smooth. Panel vans come with 136hp and 218hp motors, plus a more powerful version in the top Commerce Pro S trim. One week-long test found the 136hp motor's acceleration adequate rather than quick.

### Cab and kit

The floor is flat and the gear selector is on a stalk, so the middle passenger gets a better deal than in most vans. There's no overhead storage, because the airbags sit up in the roof. There's a 13-inch touchscreen with Apple CarPlay and Android Auto and a permanent strip of ventilation controls along the bottom, but most functions mean prodding the screen. You can add two optional 230V sockets in the load area, 2.3kW in total, but not the Ford's clever extras, such as the steering wheel that flips flat into a table.

Electric panel vans come in Commerce Plus, Commerce Pro and Commerce Pro S trims. Commerce Plus has a heat pump, heated front seats and all-season tyres, plus a reversing camera, front and rear parking sensors and keyless start. Commerce Pro adds 16-inch alloy wheels, a heated windscreen, wireless phone charging and adaptive cruise control.

### Which version to buy

Commerce Plus is the one: the 136hp motor looks enough for most buyers, and the Pro's extras are nice rather than necessary. It's also the cheapest version and has VW's longest official range.

### Price

Volkswagen's UK price list (issued 20 July 2026, with electric van prices valid from 15 January 2026) starts the e-Transporter panel van at £42,260 ex VAT before the grant, for the short-wheelbase Commerce Plus with the 136hp motor. That's £10,550 more than the cheapest diesel Transporter panel van, at £31,710. Commerce Pro starts at £44,475. VW says the panel van qualifies for the government's van grant, a discount of 35% of the price, up to £5,000, though Commerce Pro S was still awaiting approval. GOV.UK lists the e-Transporter panel van too (see our [grants and tax guide](/guides/grants-and-tax/)).

The warranty is where the VW pulls ahead: it covers the van for five years or 124,000 miles, with three services and three MOTs and five years of roadside assistance. Ford's own price list gives its van 3 years or 100,000 miles. VW covers the battery for 8 years or 100,000 miles, and says it should keep at least 70% of its original capacity over that time. Compare costs with our [electric vs diesel calculator](/tools/electric-vs-diesel/).
