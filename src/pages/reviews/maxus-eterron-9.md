The eTerron 9 is Maxus's second electric pickup, and Maxus announced it as the successor to its rear-drive T90 pickup. It's a very different machine: four-wheel drive, 442hp, standard air suspension and the biggest battery in the class. Few UK road tests have been published, so this review leans on the published figures.

### Who it's for

It suits buyers who tow horseboxes, boats or caravans, or who want an electric pickup that can cover real distances. It is really a lifestyle pickup. If your bed is full of blocks every day, it isn't the right tool.

### Load bed, payload and towing

There are two trims, Luxury and Premium. Both carry 620 kg and tow 3,500kg braked. The bed is 1,561mm long, 1,500mm wide and 535mm deep, and a party trick helps with long loads: the rear wall of the cab folds down, so the load floor runs to 2.4m. An Easy Load mode drops the bed by 60mm, and there's a 236-litre front boot. At 5.5m long, it's a big truck to park.

Maxus has promised a Utility version with leaf springs and a 1,050kg payload, but as of January 2026 its arrival was still uncertain.

### Tax: van or car?

HMRC's VAT approach sorts double cab pickups by payload: under one tonne counts as a car, a tonne or more as a van. Payload for this test is gross weight minus kerb weight, and HMRC's VAT manual says most ordinary business cars carry a block on reclaiming the input tax. At 620 kg the eTerron 9 is well under the line, so don't plan on reclaiming the VAT without advice.

For benefit in kind, HMRC says that from 6 April 2025 most double cab pickups are expected to be classified as cars, judged on the vehicle as a whole, with transitional rules for some pickups bought, leased or ordered before that date. Your own position depends on how you buy and use it, so check with your accountant. Our [grants and tax guide](/guides/grants-and-tax/) explains more.

### Range and charging

The 102kWh battery gives a 267-mile official range in most reports (one report gives 261 miles), the longest of the electric pickups we cover. Rapid charging peaks at 115kW, enough for 20 to 80 per cent in about 40 minutes. There are 2.2kW sockets in the front boot and bed, plus an external 6.6kW outlet. See our [charging guide](/guides/charging-an-electric-van/).

Off road, six preset terrain modes work with the air suspension, but we haven't seen enough testing to say how it copes.

### Alternatives

The [Isuzu D-Max EV](/vans/isuzu-d-max/) also tows 3.5 tonnes and carries over a tonne, but has a much shorter range. The [KGM Musso EV](/vans/kgm-musso/) is far cheaper, and the [Toyota Hilux electric](/vans/toyota-hilux/) has a good off-road reputation.

### Price

Reported prices are rounded: £55,000 excluding VAT in May 2026, and nearly £55,000 before VAT in March, so we haven't scored price. Only the Luxury and Premium versions are on the government's grant list, which is worth up to £5,000 on some large vans. The battery warranty has been reported as 8 years or 150,000 miles, but we couldn't confirm it with Maxus, so check it with the dealer and get a written quote.
