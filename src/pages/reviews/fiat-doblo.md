### Who it's for

The E-Doblo suits trades and delivery operators who want a small electric van for local rounds, and perhaps already deal with a Fiat Professional dealer. It shares its body, battery and motor with the [Citroën ë-Berlingo](/vans/citroen-berlingo/), [Peugeot E-Partner](/vans/peugeot-partner/), [Vauxhall Combo Electric](/vans/vauxhall-combo/) and [Toyota Proace City Electric](/vans/toyota-proace-city/). It went on sale in its current form in 2024, with a revised nose, and the cabin is the same as the others bar the badge on the steering wheel. This review covers that facelifted van only.

### Load area and payload

The load bay is identical to the diesel Doblo's. The L2 holds up to 4.4 cubic metres with the through-load bulkhead, and the L1 3.8. There's 1,229mm between the wheelarches, so two Euro pallets fit. Only the L2 gets a sliding door on each side as standard; a second sliding door is an option on the L1.

Payload runs from 642kg for the electric L2 Crew Van up to 781kg for the L1 (759kg on the Primo), which is strong for a small electric van. The trade-off shows up with a trailer: towing tops out at 750kg against 1,050kg for the diesel.

### Range and charging in the real world

Fiat's own July 2025 price and specification guide gives the WLTP combined range as 179.6 to 220 miles for the panel van and the crew van alike. The same guide also gives 222 miles in its price table and 213 miles on its technical page, so get the WLTP figure for your exact van in writing. Whatever it is, fast driving eats into it noticeably, and town work is where it does best.

Every version can rapid charge at up to 100kW, which takes it from zero to 80 per cent in half an hour. At the depot, an overnight wallbox top-up is how it's meant to be run; our [charging guide](/guides/charging-an-electric-van/) covers the options.

### On the road

Both published road tests describe it as smooth and quiet. One found it more relaxing to drive than the diesel, with less noise and vibration and no gearchanges to think about; the other found it settled at all speeds up to the national limit. It is said to lean less in corners than the diesel thanks to the low battery, and it has a tight turning circle. Reports differ on the ride with nothing in the back. One says the unladen ride is more settled than the diesel's; the other found it a little jittery when empty.

### Cab and kit

Fiat's UK price list offers the E-Doblo panel van in Plus and Primo trims. The guide's equipment tables also show a base Doblo panel van, with a footnote saying the Doblo Multijet3 100PS diesel remains available for fleets only. Plus comes with air conditioning, Bluetooth, rear parking sensors, a 10-inch touchscreen, digital instruments and a load-through bulkhead; Primo adds sat-nav and painted exterior trim. Security is thinner than it should be: a perimeter alarm is standard on Primo, a paid option on Plus, and not available on the crew van. The twin passenger seat is narrow for a third adult.

### Which version to buy

Plus already has the touchscreen and through-load bulkhead, which is enough unless your drivers want built-in sat-nav rather than navigating on their phones, though the alarm is a paid option on Plus. The standard warranty is three years or 100,000 miles, and the battery is guaranteed to keep 70% of its capacity for eight years or 100,000 miles. On top, Fiat's FlexCare plans offer an extended warranty with roadside assistance over the contract term you choose, and service care plans. A trade review reports a five-year warranty, roadside and servicing package for those prepared to pay more. Servicing is every two years or 25,000 miles, with a first inspection after a year or 12,500 miles.

### Alternatives

Start with the siblings. The Toyota offers up to 10 years of warranty if serviced annually at a Toyota dealer, the Peugeot has a small-wheel, high-dial cockpit, and the Citroën has softer front seats. Outside the family, try the [Renault Kangoo E-Tech](/vans/renault-kangoo/), [Ford E-Transit Courier](/vans/ford-transit-courier/) and [Kia PV5 Cargo](/vans/kia-pv5-cargo/).

### Price

We couldn't confirm a current list price. In March 2024 the cheapest E-Doblo was reported at £26,205 excluding VAT, after the plug-in van grant, which is too old to plan on. The E-Doblo is on the government's small-van grant list, worth up to £2,500 off at the dealer (see our [grants and tax guide](/guides/grants-and-tax/)), and our [electric vs diesel calculator](/tools/electric-vs-diesel/) will show whether it pays.
