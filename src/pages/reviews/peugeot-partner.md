### Who it's for

The E-Partner is for local trades and delivery work where the van does its miles in town and charges overnight. It's Peugeot's take on the Stellantis small van, and it shares its body, battery and motor with the [Citroën ë-Berlingo](/vans/citroen-berlingo/), [Vauxhall Combo Electric](/vans/vauxhall-combo/), [Fiat E-Doblo](/vans/fiat-doblo/) and [Toyota Proace City Electric](/vans/toyota-proace-city/). This review is about the current van, sold since the 2024 facelift.

### The cockpit question

This is the bit that makes the Peugeot different, so it comes first. The i-Cockpit gives you a small steering wheel with the dials set above it, where its sister vans use a larger wheel with the dials behind it. It's a layout some drivers like and others can't get comfortable with, and the wheel can end up lower than you'd like if you want to read the dials. It costs a little storage too: you lose the lidded cubby above the dials that the Citroën and Vauxhall have, and get a small tray instead. If the driving position doesn't suit you, look at one of the siblings.

### Load area and payload

The E-Partner offers up to 4.4 cubic metres, the same maximum as the diesel Partner. The M van holds 3.3 cubic metres and the XL 3.9, or 3.8 and 4.4 with the passenger seat folded. Peugeot's own August 2026 price guide gives the payload as up to 759kg in the M and 691kg in the XL, not counting the driver. The electric van tows a 750kg trailer at most, so heavy towing is still diesel territory.

### Range and charging in the real world

At the facelift launch the quoted figure was 205 miles. Peugeot's guide now gives an official combined figure of 213 miles, and a range of 180 to 219 miles in its WLTP table, the same for the M and XL. It suits a van doing well under 200 miles a day around its patch: lots of slow driving and regenerative braking helps, while sustained high speeds pull it down faster.

Rapid charging runs at up to 100kW, enough to go from zero to 80 per cent in half an hour. At the depot, Peugeot quotes 6 hours 43 minutes to full on a 7.4kW wallbox, or 4 hours 50 minutes on an 11kW three-phase wallbox with the £350 on-board charger upgrade, which Peugeot only recommends if you'll mainly charge from three-phase power. See our [charging guide](/guides/charging-an-electric-van/).

### On the road

The one published road test of the facelifted E-Partner we've seen found it smoother and quieter than the diesel at speed, and said the battery's weight helps settle the ride with a light load on board. Regenerative braking is set with paddles behind the wheel, though it's not strong enough for one-pedal driving. Watch the blind spots on both sides.

### Cab and kit

The panel van comes in Professional Plus and Asphalt trims, and the crew van in Professional trim. Professional Plus brings a 10-inch touchscreen with smartphone mirroring and a 10-inch digital instrument display, plus a three-seat cab with a through-load bulkhead. Asphalt adds sat-nav, a body-coloured rear bumper, fog lights and an alarm, plus a digital rear-view mirror with side view, blind spot detection and a 180-degree rear camera. Alloy wheels are an option, at £300. The facelift also arrived with ChatGPT built into its voice assistant, a novelty most trades will ignore. Three adults is a squeeze.

### Which version to buy

Professional Plus is the one we'd go for: the screens and through-load bulkhead make the most difference day to day. Servicing is every two years or 25,000 miles after a first check at 12 months or 12,500 miles. There's a basic three-year warranty, and the battery is covered for eight years or 100,000 miles.

### Alternatives

Because the five vans share so much, the decision usually comes down to the best finance deal or the most convenient place to get it serviced. Toyota's warranty can run to 10 years if the van is serviced by Toyota, which is the strongest reason to look beyond Peugeot. Outside the family, try the [Renault Kangoo E-Tech](/vans/renault-kangoo/), [Ford E-Transit Courier](/vans/ford-transit-courier/) and [Kia PV5 Cargo](/vans/kia-pv5-cargo/).

### Price

Peugeot's price guide lists the E-Partner from £28,955 + VAT for the M Professional Plus panel van, before the grant, up to £31,255 + VAT for the crew van. The E-Partner is on the government's small-van grant list, worth up to £2,500 off at the dealer; our [grants and tax guide](/guides/grants-and-tax/) explains how it works, and our [electric vs diesel calculator](/tools/electric-vs-diesel/) shows whether it pays for your mileage.
