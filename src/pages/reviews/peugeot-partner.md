### Who it's for

The E-Partner is for local trades and delivery work where the van does its miles in town and charges overnight. It's Peugeot's take on the Stellantis small van, and it shares its body, battery and motor with the [Citroën ë-Berlingo](/vans/citroen-berlingo/), [Vauxhall Combo Electric](/vans/vauxhall-combo/), [Fiat E-Doblo](/vans/fiat-doblo/) and [Toyota Proace City Electric](/vans/toyota-proace-city/). This review is about the current van, sold since the 2024 facelift.

### The cockpit question

This is the bit that makes the Peugeot different, so it comes first. The i-Cockpit gives you a small steering wheel with the dials set above it, where the other four use a larger wheel with the dials behind it. It's a layout some drivers like and others can't get comfortable with, and the wheel can end up lower than you'd like if you want to read the dials. It costs a little storage too: you lose the lidded cubby above the dials that the others have, and get a small tray instead. If the driving position doesn't suit you, buy one of the siblings. There's nothing else to lose.

### Load area and payload

The load bay is the same as the diesel's. The Standard van holds 3.3 cubic metres and the Long 3.9, or 4.4 with the through-load seat and hatch in use. At the 2024 relaunch the payload was quoted at up to 780kg, and the electric van tows a 750kg trailer at most, so heavy towing is still diesel territory.

### Range and charging in the real world

At the facelift launch, Peugeot quoted 205 miles. A 2025 review gives 213 miles, against 220 for the Citroën, so check the exact figure on your quote. It suits a van doing well under 200 miles a day around its patch: lots of slow driving and regenerative braking helps, while sustained high speeds pull it down faster.

Rapid charging runs at up to 100kW, enough to go from zero to 80 per cent in half an hour. At the depot it's seven and a half hours on a 7.4kW wallbox, or five hours at 11kW with the £350 onboard charger upgrade. See our [charging guide](/guides/charging-an-electric-van/).

### On the road

The electric version has a reputation for being smoother and quieter than the diesel at speed, and the battery's weight helps settle the ride with a light load on board. Regenerative braking is set with paddles behind the wheel, though it's not strong enough for one-pedal driving. Watch the blind spots on both sides.

### Cab and kit

The trims are Professional, Professional Plus and Asphalt. Professional Plus brings a 10-inch touchscreen with smartphone mirroring and a 10-inch digital instrument display, plus a three-seat cab with a through-load bulkhead. Asphalt adds body-coloured bumpers, alloy wheels, sat-nav and rear and nearside cameras. The facelift also arrived with ChatGPT built into its voice assistant, a novelty most trades will ignore. Three adults is a squeeze.

### Which version to buy

Professional Plus is the one we'd go for: the screens and through-load bulkhead make the most difference day to day. Servicing is every two years or 25,000 miles after a first check at 12 months or 12,500 miles. There's a basic three-year warranty, and the battery is covered for eight years or 100,000 miles.

### Alternatives

Because the five vans share so much, the decision usually comes down to the best finance deal or the most convenient place to get it serviced. Toyota's warranty can run to 10 years if the van is serviced by Toyota, which is the strongest reason to look beyond Peugeot. Outside the family, try the [Renault Kangoo E-Tech](/vans/renault-kangoo/), [Ford E-Transit Courier](/vans/ford-transit-courier/) and [Kia PV5 Cargo](/vans/kia-pv5-cargo/).

### Price

We couldn't find a current list price. In March 2024 the E-Partner was reported from £26,205 after the plug-in van grant, which is too old to rely on.
