### Who it's for

The Vivaro Electric suits a tradesperson or fleet doing local and regional work who wants a proper medium van without a diesel. This generation dates back to 2019 and was facelifted in 2024, and it is starting to show its years next to younger rivals such as the Kia PV5 Cargo. If your vans do a lot of short trips around town, the electric one makes the most sense. If they spend most of the day on the motorway, a diesel Vivaro is the better bet.

### Load area and payload

The load area is the big draw here. The standard van holds 5.3 cubic metres and the XL 6.1, rising to 5.8 and 6.6 if you use the through-load hatch in the bulkhead for planks, ladders and pipes. The XL's extra length is all behind the rear axle, so both share the same wheelbase.

Payload is the weak spot. The electric van carries up to 1,210kg, against 1,326kg for the lower-powered 2.0-litre diesel. If you carry heavy kit every day, check the figure for the exact version you're ordering. Rear access is tighter than on the old Vivaro, too: the back door openings are 100mm shorter and the rear opening 109mm narrower.

### Range and charging in the real world

The official WLTP combined range for the 75kWh battery is 219 miles. When the facelift was announced in November 2023, the quoted figure was 217 miles, 12 more than the outgoing van, so published numbers differ slightly. Either way, some electric rivals go further.

Don't plan your week around the official figure. Heavy loads and higher speeds both eat into it, and as with any electric van, winter range is much lower than summer range. Leave a healthy margin on regular routes.

Charging is a strength. A 100kW rapid charger takes the battery from 5 to 80% within 45 minutes. On AC it can take up to 11kW, but most home wallboxes are 7.4kW units, and a full charge from one of those takes around 10 hours. Our [charging guide](/guides/charging-an-electric-van/) covers the options.

### On the road

By all accounts the electric versions are the quietest and briskest Vivaros, with instant acceleration and none of a diesel's clatter. Both electric and diesel versions are said to be fairly quiet at motorway speeds. Much of the detailed road testing of the current van has been done in diesel form, which points to quick, car-like steering, good stability with a load on board and some bounce when it's empty. The Ford Transit Custom has a reputation as the more enjoyable drive. Visibility is the main gripe: there are no wide-angle door mirrors, which the old van had.

### Cab and kit

The facelift brought a 10-inch touchscreen and a 10-inch digital instrument display, with wireless smartphone connection. The climate controls are separate, which we like. Lane departure warning, adaptive cruise control and autonomous emergency braking are standard, and the Vivaro earned a gold Euro NCAP rating when tested in 2024.

The plastics are hard-wearing, but the seating position is surprisingly low, the glovebox is small and it isn't the most comfortable van for carrying three.

### Which version to buy

With the 75kWh battery, the main choice is length. Pick the standard van for tight streets and the XL for long loads. Budget for a home or depot wallbox too, because overnight charging is how this van works best. Servicing is every two years or 25,000 miles.

### Alternatives

The [Citroën ë-Dispatch](/vans/citroen-dispatch/), [Peugeot E-Expert](/vans/peugeot-expert/), [Fiat E-Scudo](/vans/fiat-scudo/) and [Toyota Proace Electric](/vans/toyota-proace/) are pretty much the same van, though the Toyota has a better warranty and prices across the five can vary considerably. The [Ford E-Transit Custom](/vans/ford-transit-custom/) is the obvious rival: the Transit Custom range offers up to 5.8 cubic metres without a through-load hatch, and 6.8 in XL form. Compare it with the newer [Kia PV5 Cargo](/vans/kia-pv5-cargo/) and the [Volkswagen e-Transporter](/vans/volkswagen-transporter/) too.

### Price

We couldn't confirm a current UK list price for the Vivaro Electric, so get a written quote. When the facelift was announced in November 2023, the electric van was cut by £2,580 to £42,055 excluding VAT. A spread of £37,075 to £58,081 was reported in May 2026, but it covers diesel and electric versions together and doesn't say whether VAT is included. The standard warranty is three years or 100,000 miles, and the battery is covered for eight years against loss of capacity.
