The ë-Relay is Citroën's badge on the big Stellantis electric van. It shares its body, electric motor and battery with the [Vauxhall Movano Electric](/vans/vauxhall-movano/), [Peugeot E-Boxer](/vans/peugeot-boxer/), [Fiat E-Ducato](/vans/fiat-ducato/) and [Toyota Proace Max Electric](/vans/toyota-proace-max/), so this review concentrates on what is different about buying the Citroën. The detailed UK road impressions we found cover the Relay range as a whole rather than a dedicated electric test.

### What's changed

Until the end of 2024, the electric Relay was converted by an outside company, while Fiat built its own E-Ducato. That has gone. The current ë-Relay uses the same electric motor and battery as the rest of the family, a 110kWh pack under the load floor. If you're looking at a used electric Relay, check which version it is: older ones are a different van underneath.

### Load area and payload

Citroën's October 2026 price guide lists five electric panel vans: an L3H2 at 3.5 tonnes, and L3H2, L3H3, L4H2 and L4H3 vans at 4.25 tonnes. There's also an L4H2 Glazed Van, an L3H1 Chassis Cab and a Dropside. Because the electric van has the same internal dimensions as the diesel, load volume runs from 13 cubic metres in the L3H2 to 17 in the L4H3. Every van is 2,050mm wide without mirrors, which matters on narrow streets.

Payload is the catch. At 3.5 tonnes the electric van carries just 730kg, while the 4.25-tonne versions carry 1,385kg to 1,480kg. Citroën says these figures are worked out without a driver, so the driver's weight counts against them, as does any optional kit.

You may read that the heavier van needs a commercial licence. Under the current GOV.UK rules, a standard category B car licence covers electric vehicles up to 4,250kg, including one gained on or after 1 January 1997 (the limit for petrol and diesel vans is 3,500kg). So the 4.25-tonne ë-Relay can be driven on a car licence.

### Range and charging

Citroën's guide gives up to 263 miles for the 4.25-tonne L3H2 and 235 miles for the 3.5-tonne van. These are worked out using the WLTP method, because the ë-Relay is homologated as a heavy vehicle and it isn't tested under WLTP itself. Rapid charging at 150kW takes the battery from 5 to 80% in 55 minutes. There's a 22kW on-board charger, so a 22kW three-phase wallbox fills it in about six hours, but a 7.4kW wallbox takes a little under 17 hours from flat. The charging socket sits behind a flap by the passenger door, where the diesel's filler is. Our [charging guide](/guides/charging-an-electric-van/) covers depot and home options.

### On the road

The road impressions we found come from one review of the whole Relay range, diesel included. In it, an empty Relay, especially the diesel, can bounce over poor roads. The battery's weight under the floor helps settle the ride in the electric van, and it rates the electric van as more refined, but wind and road noise are still there. Brake energy recovery is smooth, which helps keep loads in place. Some drivers find the seat feels pushed forward to free up cargo space.

### Cab and kit

The electric panel van comes in one trim, Enterprise, with automatic air conditioning, rear parking sensors, an electric parking brake, a seven-inch touchscreen with Apple CarPlay and Android Auto, and Citroën's connected services. Citroën's trim page says cruise control isn't fitted to the 4.25-tonne vans, which get blind spot and moving off warnings, a tachograph and a 56mph limiter instead. There's storage under the passenger seat for the charging cable. Side airbags cost £540 before VAT and need the £350 Interior Upgrade Pack.

### Warranty and price

The van warranty is three years or 100,000 miles, paint cover is two years and there's a five-year anti-perforation guarantee. The electric van's cover is more generous: the battery and running gear get eight years or 100,000 miles, and Citroën's guide says the battery is covered down to 70% of its charging capacity. Toyota's warranty can run to 10 years if the van is serviced every year at a Toyota dealer, though.

Citroën's October 2026 guide lists the ë-Relay panel van from £49,735 before VAT for the 3.5-tonne L3H2, up to £52,165 for the L4H3 4.25T. Its on-the-road prices, which include delivery and number plates and take off the £5,000 van grant, start at £46,224.17 before VAT. The ë-Relay is on the government's list of large vans eligible for the van grant, worth up to £5,000 (our [grants and tax guide](/guides/grants-and-tax/) explains how it works).
