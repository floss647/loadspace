Farizon is the commercial vehicle brand of Geely, and the SV is a purpose-built electric van rather than a converted diesel. It's on sale in the UK now, imported by Jameel Motors, through a short list of dealers that includes Rygor in Reading and Oxford, Waterton in Bridgend and Pentagon in Derby, while the AA handles servicing and repairs. It's on the government's approved list for the higher, large-van rate of the Plug-in Van Grant (see our [grants guide](/guides/grants-and-tax/)).

### Who it's for

The SV straddles two classes. With five body sizes from 6.95 to 13.0 cubic metres, the smallest rivals the [Ford E-Transit Custom](/reviews/ford-transit-custom/) and the biggest takes on the [Ford E-Transit](/reviews/ford-transit/). It suits trades who want a heavy payload and lots of standard kit, and who don't mind buying from a young brand. Only the L1H1, at 1,980mm tall, gets under 2m.

### Load area and payload

Payload is the headline. The L1H1 with the 67kWh battery carries 1,350 kg, falling to 1,265 kg with the 83kWh battery, and the L3H3 still carries 1,045 kg or 1,035 kg. Every version can tow 2,000 kg. The load floor sits at 550mm, and there's no B-pillar on the nearside, so the side door and passenger door open into one wide gap. In a panel van a bulkhead cancels much of the benefit, and the passenger's seatbelt is mounted in the door. A built-in payload monitor shows the van's weight, but treat it as a guide, not a weighbridge.

### Range and charging in the real world

There are 67kWh and 83kWh LFP batteries, or a 106kWh NMC battery on the L3H3 only. Official ranges run from 188 miles for the L1H1 67kWh and 234 miles for the L1H1 83kWh to 247 miles for the L3H3 106kWh. The 83kWh pack charges at up to 140kW and the other two at 120kW, and Farizon quotes a 20 to 80 per cent charge in 36 minutes. AC charging is up to 11kW. In one road test, an unladen 83kWh van averaged 2.6 miles per kWh, or around 215 miles from a charge. More in our [charging guide](/guides/charging-an-electric-van/).

### On the road

Only a handful of UK road tests have been published, and they don't agree. Some found it pleasant and composed; one longer test on UK roads reported a firm, unsettled ride, poor traction in the wet and inconsistent regenerative braking. On one test van the steer-by-wire system kept making small corrections until lane-keeping assistance was switched off. Reports do agree that the warning chimes are intrusive.

### Cab and kit

The standard SV is well stocked, with heated and ventilated front seats, a heated steering wheel, a 360-degree camera and physical climate controls. Reports on cabin quality differ. For 2026 Farizon added a six-way driver's seat, a steering wheel that now adjusts four ways and wireless Android Auto, which answers some early complaints, plus four years of AA roadside assistance. There's a four-year, 120,000-mile vehicle warranty; the battery is covered for eight years, but sources give different mileage limits, so get the terms in writing.

### Price

In April 2026 Farizon added a new entry-level Core trim, an L1H1 only, which drops the payload monitor, vehicle-to-load power, automatic wipers and heated windscreen. Prices then were £39,000 for the L1H1 Core with the 67kWh battery, £44,000 for the standard L1H1 and £55,000 for the L3H3 with the 106kWh battery, ex VAT and before the grant. An August 2026 road test of the L3H3 gave the same £55,000 starting figure, without saying whether it includes VAT. We haven't found a full price list from the past six months, so get a written quote, and compare running costs with our [electric vs diesel tool](/tools/electric-vs-diesel/).

### Alternatives

The [Renault Master E-Tech](/reviews/renault-master/) and [Maxus eDeliver 9](/vans/maxus-deliver-9/) are worth a look alongside the Fords.
