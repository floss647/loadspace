Farizon is the commercial vehicle brand of Geely, and the SV is a purpose-built electric van rather than a converted diesel. It's on sale in the UK now. The brand was launched here by the importer Jameel Motors, and recent announcements come from Farizon Auto UK. The dealer network is still young: dealers named in fleet deals this year include Rygor in Reading and Oxford, Waterton in Bridgend and Pentagon in Derby. Since launch the AA has been Farizon's partner for servicing and repairs, including mobile servicing. It's on the government's approved list for the higher, large-van rate of the Plug-in Van Grant (see our [grants guide](/guides/grants-and-tax/)).

### Who it's for

The SV straddles two classes. With five body sizes from 6.95 to 13.0 cubic metres, the smallest is closest to the [Ford E-Transit Custom](/reviews/ford-transit-custom/) and the biggest is in [Ford E-Transit](/reviews/ford-transit/) territory. It suits trades who want a heavy payload and lots of standard kit, and who don't mind buying from a young brand. Only the L1H1, at 1,980mm tall, gets under 2m.

### Load area and payload

Payload is the headline. The L1H1 with the 67kWh battery carries 1,350 kg, falling to 1,265 kg with the 83kWh battery, and the L3H3 still carries 1,045 kg or 1,035 kg (83kWh or 106kWh battery, driver included). Every version can tow 2,000 kg. The load floor sits at 550mm, and there's no B-pillar on the nearside, so the side door and passenger door open into one wide gap. In a panel van a bulkhead cancels much of the benefit, and the passenger's seatbelt is mounted in the door. A built-in payload monitor (not on the Core trim) shows the van's weight, but treat it as a guide, not a weighbridge.

### Range and charging in the real world

There are 67kWh and 83kWh LFP batteries, or a 106kWh NMC battery on the L3H3 only. The L1H1 is rated at 188 miles with the 67kWh pack and 234 miles with the 83kWh, and the L3H3 106kWh at 247 miles, the longest official (WLTP) range in the line-up; bigger bodies with the smaller packs are rated lower. The 83kWh pack charges at up to 140kW and the other two at 120kW, and Farizon quotes a 20 to 80 per cent charge in 36 minutes for the 67kWh and 83kWh packs and 40 minutes for the 106kWh. AC charging is up to 11kW. In one road test, an unladen 83kWh van averaged 2.6 miles per kWh, which that test put at around 215 miles from a charge. More in our [charging guide](/guides/charging-an-electric-van/).

### On the road

Only a handful of road tests have been published, and they're split. Early drives found it pleasant, composed and smooth over bumps. One test on UK roads, published in January 2026, reported a firm, unsettled ride, poor traction in the wet and inconsistent regenerative braking. That test predates the 2026 model-year updates, which Farizon described mainly as changes to the cab, connectivity, security and kit. On another test van the steer-by-wire system kept making small corrections until lane-keeping assistance was switched off. Two tests found the warning chimes shrill or insistent.

### Cab and kit

The standard SV is well stocked, with heated and ventilated front seats, a heated steering wheel, a 360-degree camera and physical climate controls. Reports on cabin quality differ. For 2026 Farizon added a six-way driver's seat, a steering wheel that now adjusts four ways and wireless Android Auto, which answers some early complaints, plus four years of AA roadside assistance. There's a four-year, 120,000-mile vehicle warranty; the battery is covered for eight years, but the mileage limit has been reported as 160,000 miles in one launch report and 120,000 miles in other reviews, so get the terms in writing.

### Price

In April 2026 Farizon added a new entry-level Core trim, an L1H1 only, which drops the payload monitor, vehicle-to-load power, automatic wipers and heated windscreen. Prices then were £39,000 for the L1H1 Core with the 67kWh battery, £44,000 for the standard L1H1 and £55,000 for the L3H3 with the 106kWh battery, ex VAT and before the grant. The same list had the L3H3 with the 83kWh battery at £52,000. An August 2026 road test of the 106kWh L3H3 quoted £55,000 on the road, without saying whether that includes VAT. We haven't found a full price list from the past six months, so get a written quote, and compare running costs with our [electric vs diesel tool](/tools/electric-vs-diesel/).
