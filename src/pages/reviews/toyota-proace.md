### Who it's for

The Proace Electric is Toyota's badge on the Stellantis medium van, the same family as the [Citroën ë-Dispatch](/vans/citroen-dispatch/), [Peugeot E-Expert](/vans/peugeot-expert/), [Vauxhall Vivaro Electric](/vans/vauxhall-vivaro/) and [Fiat E-Scudo](/vans/fiat-scudo/). Buyers' guides list it as part of the same family, so for the shared body, cab and drivetrain, read our [Vivaro Electric](/reviews/vauxhall-vivaro/) and [ë-Dispatch](/reviews/citroen-dispatch/) reviews. The Toyota is for the operator who keeps vans a long time and wants a manufacturer warranty for as long as possible.

We found no published UK road test of the current electric Proace, and we haven't driven it, so this review sticks to Toyota's own figures and what is published.

### Is it still on sale?

Yes, but with a gap in the paperwork. Toyota is advertising finance offers on Proace Electric orders placed between 1 October and 15 December 2026, yet the Proace pages in the price list Toyota issued from 1 October 2026 list only diesel vans. Ask the dealer for a current electric price list before you get too far.

### The warranty

This is the main reason to choose the Toyota. The standard cover is three years or 60,000 miles. After that, each qualifying service at a Toyota dealer adds a further 12 months or 10,000 miles, until the van is 10 years old or has covered 100,000 miles, and it can even be picked up on a used Toyota van that is out of warranty. The catch is that you have to keep using Toyota dealers, and a 2026 review of the diesel rates Toyota servicing as not the cheapest. New Toyota vans also get five years of free roadside assistance.

In September 2024, Toyota's UK press office said the high-voltage battery on its electric vans is covered for eight years or 100,000 miles. We found no newer figure, and Toyota's service-activated Battery Care cover, offered on its other electric models, excludes the Proace, so confirm the battery terms in writing.

### Range, charging and kit

In February 2024 Toyota launched the facelifted diesel Proace, with a 10-inch touchscreen even on the entry Active trim. Toyota's current site shows the electric van in Icon and Sport trims, with 50kWh and 75kWh batteries.

Toyota quotes up to 217 miles with the 75kWh battery, which it says is the maximum official (WLTP) figure; its own contract-hire offer gives 133 miles for the 50kWh Icon, so the battery choice matters. On charging, Toyota quotes 0 to 80% in about 32 minutes on DC in one place and 20 to 80% in as little as 30 minutes in another, but doesn't give a kW rating. The earlier van had 100kW rapid charging, so ask what the current one takes. Our [charging guide](/guides/charging-an-electric-van/) explains what to look for.

Toyota gives the electric van a payload of 1,000kg to 1,300kg, depending on version, and towing is rated at 1,000kg. Ask for the plated payload of the exact van you're quoted.

### Price and alternatives

We couldn't confirm a list price. The only figure Toyota publishes is in that contract-hire offer, which shows the 50kWh Icon with ply lining at £37,128.33 plus VAT, but it doesn't say whether that is the cheapest version or whether the grant has been taken off. Two 2025 reviews say the Toyota tends to be pricier because of its warranty, so get quotes on all five badges and set the extra cost against the longer cover. It is on the government's list of large vans eligible for the plug-in van grant, which is worth up to £5,000; see our [grants guide](/guides/grants-and-tax/). Outside the family, compare the [Ford E-Transit Custom](/vans/ford-transit-custom/) and [Kia PV5 Cargo](/vans/kia-pv5-cargo/), and use our [electric vs diesel calculator](/tools/electric-vs-diesel/) to check the running costs.
