The eCitan is the Mercedes-Benz version of the Renault Kangoo E-Tech. It shares its bodyshell and running gear with the Renault, including a 45kWh battery and a single motor, and the [Nissan Townstar EV](/vans/nissan-townstar/) is the third twin. Mercedes gives it its own interior and a different aftersales package, and it has some of its own suspension parts. Few UK road tests of the eCitan have been published, so this review leans mostly on published figures, chiefly Mercedes' January 2025 UK price list, the latest we have. Treat its figures as a guide and confirm them with a dealer.

### Who it's for

Fleets and businesses that want a Mercedes on the drive, or already deal with a Mercedes van dealer for bigger vans. It suits local work: Mercedes' list gives the L1 BASE van 176 miles on the WLTP combined test and the L2 versions 165 to 168 miles, where Renault quoted 186 miles for the 45kWh Kangoo. Mercedes called its figures preliminary.

### Load area and payload

There are two lengths. In Mercedes' list the L1 van has 2.9 cubic metres, less than the 3.3 cubic metres of the standard Kangoo, and the L2 van 3.62 cubic metres. Payloads in the list are modest: 428kg to 448kg for the L1 and 593kg to 616kg for the L2, worked out with a 75kg driver included. A July 2026 guide gave a figure just over 700kg for all three twins without saying which version, so ask the dealer for the plated figure. It can tow up to 1,450kg, a little less than the Kangoo's 1,500kg.

### Range and charging

Reports differ on rapid charging. Mercedes' January 2025 list gives 75kW DC charging, with 10 to 80 per cent in 38 minutes, but a May 2025 review said 80kW and 0 to 80 per cent in about 35 minutes, so check with the dealer. On AC, a full charge takes about four and a half hours on an 11kW wallbox and two and a half on 22kW. Mercedes lists 22kW AC charging as standard; the May 2025 review called it an option. See our [charging guide](/guides/charging-an-electric-van/).

### On the road

The one full UK road test we found described it as smooth, quiet and easy to drive, with a comfortable ride, and a brief side-by-side comparison in a Kangoo long-term test found the two feel identical on the road. There are only three drive settings (D-, D and D+), fewer than in bigger Mercedes electric vans.

### Cab and kit

This is where the money goes. Mercedes' January 2025 list has driver and co-driver airbags, front side and window airbags, Active Brake Assist, Active Lane Keeping Assist, Attention Assist, Traffic Sign Assist, a reversing camera and a heated windscreen on every panel van, plus a 9.5-inch MBUX touchscreen. PRO adds Blind Spot Assist, and SELECT adds LED headlamps and Active Parking Assist. Automatic co-driver airbag deactivation is an extra-cost option, and the alarm isn't Thatcham-approved. Reports differ on the MBUX system, from dated to slick. Trims are BASE, PRO and SELECT, plus a five-seat Crew van, replacing the Progressive and Premium trims of launch.

### Warranty and servicing

Mercedes' January 2025 list gives a three-year unlimited-mileage van warranty and an eight-year or 100,000-mile battery warranty. That's better than Renault for high-mileage drivers, but Nissan gives the Townstar EV five years. The list also includes four services (two minor and two major), and servicing is every year or 25,000 miles. Ask whether those terms still apply.
