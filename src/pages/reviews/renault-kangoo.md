The Kangoo E-Tech is the electric version of Renault's small van, and it's the van the [Nissan Townstar EV](/vans/nissan-townstar/) and [Mercedes-Benz eCitan](/vans/mercedes-benz-citan/) are based on. Renault builds it in France, at Maubeuge, and the three share their underpinnings and the same body. So the real question is which badge gives you the best price, warranty and dealer.

### Who it's for

Trades, couriers and service fleets doing local and regional work. It's at its best around town, and long motorway days aren't its strength.

### Load area and payload

There are two lengths: the standard ML has 3.3 cubic metres and the longer LL has 4.2 cubic metres, with a sliding door on each side. Oddly, the longer van carries more, because it gets a heavier-duty rear axle that the short electric van can't have.

Renault's UK brochure gives a maximum payload of 608kg for the ML E-Tech and 764kg for the LL E-Tech, without saying whether that allows for a driver, so check the plated figure on the exact van. It can tow up to 1,500kg.

UK vans miss out on the pillarless side opening sold in left-hand-drive markets, and there's no through-load bulkhead for lengths of pipe or timber.

### Range and charging

Renault's UK brochure, dated 1 July 2026, lists the Kangoo E-Tech with a 45kWh battery, a 120hp motor, up to 190 miles on the official WLTP test and DC charging at up to 80kW, with a 20 to 80 per cent charge taking 40 minutes on an 80kW public fast charger.

In October 2026 Renault announced an updated Kangoo E-Tech with a 52kWh battery, up from 45kWh, a choice of 135hp or 155hp motors and 100kW DC charging, with 15 to 80 per cent in 30 minutes. Renault gave a provisional range of up to 354km (about 220 miles), pending homologation, depending on the version, and said prices would follow in the coming weeks. The updated van isn't in Renault's UK brochure yet, so check which van you're quoted. Renault also announced vehicle-to-load (V2L) power, but we couldn't confirm it on UK vans, or whether the Nissan and Mercedes versions get the same upgrade.

On the old 45kWh van, a long-term test managed around 130 miles in winter, dipping to about 100 miles in freezing weather, and around 140 miles as spring arrived. Expect more from the new battery, but we haven't seen tested figures. A heat pump is standard, which helps in the cold. More in our [charging guide](/guides/charging-an-electric-van/).

### On the road

Published road tests so far are of the older 45kWh van, so treat what follows as a guide to the new one. The Kangoo E-Tech has a reputation for being smooth, easy to drive and comfortable, with a quiet motor, though reports differ on how much road noise gets in at speed. The brake energy recovery is gentle: even the strongest setting won't bring it to a stop, so there's no one-pedal driving. Renault says the updated van gets four levels of regenerative braking, up from three, chosen with paddles on the steering wheel, but no road test has yet said how strong the top setting is.

### Cab and kit

On the older van, the climate dials and steering wheel come from the Clio, and the cabin is hard-wearing grey and black plastic rather than plush. A June 2024 update made a reversing camera, an electric parking brake and a digital driver's display standard. Renault's September 2026 price guide lists the E-Tech panel van in Advance and Extra trims. One long-term test of the older van found it kept dropping its Apple CarPlay connection; Renault says the updated van gets a new, more responsive 10-inch screen.

### Warranty

This is where the Renault badge loses out. The van warranty is three years or 100,000 miles, extendable at extra cost, while Nissan gives the Townstar EV five years as standard. The battery is covered for eight years or 100,000 miles. Servicing is every two years or 24,000 miles.

### Which version to buy

The long LL. It has more space and more payload, and the government's grant list puts the long-wheelbase Kangoo E-Tech on its large-van list, with a discount of up to £5,000, while the medium-wheelbase version is on the small-van list at up to £2,500. ### Price

Renault's UK price guide, valid from 1 September 2026, starts the Kangoo E-Tech at £26,850 before VAT for the ML19 E-Tech Advance panel van, or £30,835 on the road after a £2,500 van grant. The long LL21 E-Tech Advance panel van is £29,850 before VAT, or £31,935 on the road after a £5,000 grant. The on-the-road prices include VAT. The price guide doesn't say which battery these vans have, but it took effect before Renault announced the 52kWh van in October and said its prices would follow, so these are most likely prices for the 45kWh van.
