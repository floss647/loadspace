### Who it's for

The ë-Berlingo suits a sole trader, florist, courier or local fleet whose work happens mostly within a town or two, with a van that sleeps on a charger. It's the Citroën version of the Stellantis small van, and it shares its body, battery and motor with the [Peugeot E-Partner](/vans/peugeot-partner/), [Vauxhall Combo Electric](/vans/vauxhall-combo/), [Fiat E-Doblo](/vans/fiat-doblo/) and [Toyota Proace City Electric](/vans/toyota-proace-city/). This review covers the facelifted van, updated in 2024, not the earlier ë-Berlingo.

So the useful question isn't whether it's good (the platform is proven) but why you'd choose the Citroën. The answer is the seats. Citroën fits its own design, with denser foam and thicker side bolsters than the Fiat, Peugeot and Vauxhall versions. If you spend eight hours a day behind the wheel, that's a real reason to pick it.

### Load area and payload

Going electric costs you nothing in space. The M van holds 3.3 cubic metres and the longer XL 3.9, and the optional through-load hatch takes items up to 3.1 metres long, or 3.4 metres in the XL. There's 1,229mm between the wheelarches, enough for two Euro pallets side by side.

Payload tops out at 781 kg, which is strong for a small electric van. If you carry heavy loads every day, check the figure for the exact length and trim you're ordering.

### Range and charging in the real world

We couldn't pin down a single official range. A 2025 review gives Citroën's official WLTP figure as 220 miles for every version, while a July 2026 buying guide gives 220 miles in its table and 213 miles in its write-up. Check the figure on your quote. Either way, faster driving really blunts the range, so a van that lives on the motorway will fall well short.

A 100kW rapid charger takes the battery from zero to 80 per cent in about half an hour. A 7.4kW wallbox fills it from flat in seven and a half hours, and 11kW three-phase charging, a £350 option, cuts that to just over five. There's also a £750 Winter Pack with a heat pump, worth a look if you work through the cold months. Our [charging guide](/guides/charging-an-electric-van/) covers home and depot set-ups.

### On the road

By all accounts it is considerably more relaxing than the diesel on faster roads, quieter and with less vibration, and it rides well even with nothing in the back because the battery sits low between the axles. Regenerative braking is now set with paddles behind the wheel, though even the strongest setting won't bring it to a stop by itself. Visibility is the weak point: the mirrors are narrow, leaving big blind spots on both sides.

### Cab and kit

There are three trims: Enterprise, Enterprise Plus and Driver. Every version gets air conditioning, rear parking sensors, cruise control and lane-keep assist. Enterprise Plus adds a through-load bulkhead, twin passenger seats and a pair of 10-inch screens, and Driver adds navigation, rain-sensing wipers and a digital rear-view mirror with side and 180-degree rear cameras. The middle seat is narrow, so treat it as a two-seater with occasional space for a third.

### Which version to buy

Enterprise Plus is the sweet spot for most trades, because the through-load bulkhead earns its keep with pipes and lengths of timber. Servicing is every two years or 25,000 miles. The van itself gets three years or 100,000 miles of warranty, and the battery eight years or 100,000 miles, covering it down to 70 per cent of its capacity.

### Alternatives

The obvious ones are its siblings. The Peugeot has Peugeot's small-wheel, high-dial cockpit, which you should try before you buy; the Vauxhall and Fiat are all but identical to the Citroën; and the Toyota offers a potential 10-year warranty if you keep it serviced at a Toyota dealer. Outside the family, look at the [Renault Kangoo E-Tech](/vans/renault-kangoo/), [Nissan Townstar](/vans/nissan-townstar/), [Ford E-Transit Courier](/vans/ford-transit-courier/) and the bigger [Kia PV5 Cargo](/vans/kia-pv5-cargo/).

### Price

We couldn't confirm a current list price before the grant. A July 2026 buying guide put it at £27,405 excluding VAT, after the plug-in van grant, but the same guide also says it starts from under £29,000 excluding VAT, and the two don't square, so get a written quote. The ë-Berlingo is on the government's list for the small-van grant, a discount of up to £2,500 that the dealer takes off the price (see our [grants and tax guide](/guides/grants-and-tax/)).
