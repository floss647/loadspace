### Who it's for

The ë-Berlingo suits a sole trader, florist, courier or local fleet whose work happens mostly within a town or two, with a van that sleeps on a charger. It's the Citroën version of the Stellantis small van, and it shares its body, battery and motor with the [Peugeot E-Partner](/vans/peugeot-partner/), [Vauxhall Combo Electric](/vans/vauxhall-combo/), [Fiat E-Doblo](/vans/fiat-doblo/) and [Toyota Proace City Electric](/vans/toyota-proace-city/). This review covers the facelifted van, updated in 2024, not the earlier ë-Berlingo.

So the useful question isn't whether it's good (the platform is proven) but why you'd choose the Citroën. The answer is the seats. Citroën fits its own design, with denser foam and thicker side bolsters than the Fiat, Peugeot and Vauxhall versions. If you spend eight hours a day behind the wheel, that's a real reason to pick it.

### Load area and payload

The M van holds 3.3 cubic metres and the longer XL 3.9, and the optional through-load hatch takes items up to 3.1 metres long, or 3.4 metres in the XL. There's 1,229mm between the wheelarches, and room for two Euro pallets in the back.

Payload tops out at 759kg in the M panel van and 691kg in the XL, and Citroën's figures don't include the driver. That's strong for a small electric van. If you carry heavy loads every day, check the figure for the exact length and trim you're ordering.

### Range and charging in the real world

Citroën's own August 2026 price guide gives the official WLTP range as 180 to 219 miles, the same for the M and XL, depending on specification. Its technical table also lists a single combined figure of 213 miles. Either way, faster driving really blunts the range, so a van that lives on the motorway will fall well short.

A 100kW rapid charger takes the battery from zero to 80 per cent in about half an hour. A 7.4kW wallbox fills it in 6 hours 43 minutes, and the optional 11kW three-phase charger, at £350, cuts that to 4 hours 50 minutes. Citroën only recommends it if you'll mostly charge from three-phase power. On the Driver there's also a £750 Winter Pack with a heat pump, worth a look if you work through the cold months. Our [charging guide](/guides/charging-an-electric-van/) covers home and depot set-ups.

### On the road

The one published road test of the facelifted van we've seen found it considerably more relaxing than the diesel on faster roads, quieter and with less vibration, and said it rides well even with nothing in the back because the battery sits low between the axles. Regenerative braking is now set with paddles behind the wheel, though even the strongest setting won't bring it to a stop by itself. The same test found visibility the weak point: the mirrors are narrow, leaving big blind spots on both sides.

### Cab and kit

The electric panel van comes in two trims, Enterprise Plus and Driver, and the crew van in Enterprise trim. Every version gets air conditioning, rear parking sensors, cruise control and lane-keep assist. Enterprise Plus has a folding twin passenger seat with a through-load hatch in the bulkhead, a 10-inch touchscreen and 10-inch digital dials, and Driver adds navigation, rain-sensing wipers and a digital rear-view mirror with side and 180-degree rear cameras. The middle seat is narrow, so treat it as a two-seater with occasional space for a third.

### Which version to buy

Enterprise Plus is the sweet spot for most trades, because the through-load bulkhead earns its keep with pipes and lengths of timber. Servicing is every two years or 25,000 miles. The van itself gets three years or 100,000 miles of warranty, and the battery eight years or 100,000 miles, covering it down to 70 per cent of its capacity.

### Alternatives

The obvious ones are its siblings. The Peugeot has Peugeot's small-wheel, high-dial cockpit, which you should try before you buy; the Vauxhall and Fiat are all but identical to the Citroën; and the Toyota offers a potential 10-year warranty if you keep it serviced at a Toyota dealer. Outside the family, look at the [Renault Kangoo E-Tech](/vans/renault-kangoo/), [Nissan Townstar](/vans/nissan-townstar/), [Ford E-Transit Courier](/vans/ford-transit-courier/) and the bigger [Kia PV5 Cargo](/vans/kia-pv5-cargo/).

### Price

Citroën's August 2026 price list starts the ë-Berlingo panel van at £28,955 excluding VAT for the M Enterprise Plus, before the plug-in van grant, so get a written quote. The ë-Berlingo is on the government's list for the small-van grant, a discount of up to £2,500 that the dealer takes off the price (see our [grants and tax guide](/guides/grants-and-tax/)).
