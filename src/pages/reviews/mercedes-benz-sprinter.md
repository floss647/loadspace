### Who it's for

The eSprinter is Mercedes' large electric panel van, for fleets and parcel operators who need a lot of space and a long range and can stomach a big bill. The current eSprinter arrived in 2024 and is a very different van from the one sold from 2020, which managed just 96 miles. Few UK road tests of the current electric van have been published yet, so this review leans mainly on the published figures. Bear in mind too that a next-generation Sprinter is due later in 2027, with its own electric platform.

### Load area and payload

The L3 van tested in 2024 has 14 cubic metres of load space, with a maximum load length of 4,410mm and 1,350mm between the wheelarches. A buying guide updated in November 2025 lists load volumes of 9.0 to 14.0 cubic metres across the range.

Payload is the catch. Big batteries are heavy, so Mercedes rates every 113kWh van at 4.25 tonnes gross weight. Payload across the range runs from 495kg to 1,278kg, and the best figure needs the 4,250kg rating. Get the figure for the exact van you're ordering in writing.

Early reports warned that the heavier rating put it beyond a standard car licence. GOV.UK's licence rules say a category B licence (test passed on or after 1 January 1997) covers electric vehicles up to 4,250kg maximum authorised mass. More in our [van sizes guide](/guides/van-sizes-explained/).

### Range and charging in the real world

The 113kWh battery has an official WLTP combined range of 272 miles: at launch, more than any other large van promised. Published figures do vary a little, from 271 miles in the 2024 road test to 279 miles in a September 2026 report, so check the figure for your spec. The 81kWh battery is rated at up to 185 miles.

On real-world range, the only measured figure we found is from the 2024 test, where an hour-long hilly route with a light load returned 3.1 miles per kWh, and there's no published figure with a heavy load.

Rapid charging runs at up to 115kW, taking the 113kWh battery from 10 to 80% in 42 minutes, but public chargers that fast tend to be expensive. On AC it takes 11 hours on an 11kW wallbox and around 17 hours on a 7kW supply, so this is a van to charge overnight at base. See our [charging guide](/guides/charging-an-electric-van/).

### On the road

The one detailed UK road test of the current van reports it smooth, comfortable and easy to drive. It found the new D Auto regeneration mode rarely seemed to pick the right setting. There's a choice of 136hp and 204hp motors, with little difference between the two on the road. One test isn't enough for us to call any of this a reputation.

### Cab and kit

The cab has a 10.25-inch MBUX touchscreen with wireless Apple CarPlay and Android Auto and over-the-air updates. There are two trims, Pro and Select. Pro gets a heated driver's seat, air conditioning, pre-entry climate control, a full-height bulkhead and load bay lining. Safety kit listed as standard includes Active Brake Assist, Blind Spot Assist, Crosswind Assist, Active Lane Keeping Assist and a reversing camera. The Sprinter's Euro NCAP Silver rating comes from a diesel van. Check the 2024 kit lists against a current quote.

### Which version to buy

Take the 81kWh battery unless your routes need the extra range; it is far cheaper. Pro trim is enough for most fleets. The van warranty is three years with unlimited mileage, the battery is covered for eight years or 100,000 miles, and servicing is every 12 months or 40,000 miles.

### Alternatives

Compare it with the [Ford E-Transit](/vans/ford-transit/), the [Renault Master E-Tech](/vans/renault-master/), which won Parkers Large Van of the Year 2026, the [Vauxhall Movano Electric](/vans/vauxhall-movano/) and its Stellantis twins, the [Iveco eDaily](/vans/iveco-daily/) and the [Maxus eDeliver 9](/vans/maxus-deliver-9/).

### Price

We couldn't open Mercedes' own UK site, so get a written quote. A November 2025 buying guide listed the 81kWh van from £59,915 and the 113kWh van from £73,260, both excluding VAT and before the grant. The eSprinter is on the GOV.UK list of large vans eligible for the Plug-in Van Grant, where the maximum discount is £5,000 (see our [grants and tax guide](/guides/grants-and-tax/)).
