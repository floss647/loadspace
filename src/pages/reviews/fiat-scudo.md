### What's different about the Fiat

The E-Scudo is Fiat Professional's badge on the Stellantis medium electric van, with the same body and electric drivetrain as the [Citroën ë-Dispatch](/vans/citroen-dispatch/), [Peugeot E-Expert](/vans/peugeot-expert/), [Vauxhall Vivaro Electric](/vans/vauxhall-vivaro/) and [Toyota Proace Electric](/vans/toyota-proace/). What changes is a different nose and some equipment changes. Fiat's own pitch is about storage, so expect plenty of cubbies, but the bigger differences for a buyer are price, trim names and the warranty. It suits sole traders and fleets running mostly urban and suburban rounds.

### Load area and payload

The L1 holds 5.3 cubic metres and the L2 6.1, and a through-load hatch stretches the L1's floor length from 2,512mm to 3,674mm for pipes and lengths of timber. The side door openings are 935mm wide, which is modest for the class, though you get one on each side as standard.

We couldn't find a current maximum payload for the E-Scudo panel van. A 2026 buyers' guide puts the whole family at payloads of up to 1,000kg, and the E-Scudo Crew Van has the lowest payload in the range at 915kg, so ask for the plated figure on the exact van. One more catch: the electric version can tow up to a tonne, but Fiat doesn't sell a tow bar for it.

### Range and charging in the real world

Range figures for the Fiat don't quite line up. The 2024 launch information gave 217 miles from the 75kWh battery, and Fiat now quotes 219. That's about ten miles fewer than Peugeot quotes for the E-Expert, even though the two are pretty much the same van. A 2025 review also lists a 50kWh version with a 139-mile range, which the Peugeot doesn't offer, but the launch information only mentioned the bigger battery, so check availability with a dealer. As with any electric van, range falls at motorway speeds.

A 100kW rapid charger takes the 75kWh battery from 5 to 80% in 45 minutes. At home, a 7.4kW wallbox needs 11 hours 20 minutes, and 11kW charging is a £400 option. Our [charging guide](/guides/charging-an-electric-van/) covers what to install.

### On the road

We haven't driven it, and the one published UK road test we found (from 2025) rates the E-Scudo the pick of the Scudo range to drive: smoother and quieter than the diesels, and its 134bhp is plenty at low speeds but runs short at higher ones. The regenerative braking is adjustable but not strong enough for one-pedal driving. The same test found grabby brakes and numb steering across the Scudo range, and a smallish windscreen limits the view out, so the optional camera kit is worth a look.

### Cab and kit

Fiat calls its trims Scudo, Primo and Onyx. The base van gets a 10-inch touchscreen with smartphone connection, parking sensors, sliding doors on both sides, solid rear doors, 16-inch steel wheels and automatic emergency braking. A 2025 review says Primo adds 17-inch wheels, painted bumpers, the through-load bulkhead, sat-nav and an alarm. Fiat's March 2024 launch details also listed Dynamic Surround View with a digital rear-view mirror and side view, and blind-spot detection, on Primo, so check the current Primo spec sheet for those. Onyx is a limited edition with a body kit, rally-style graphics, keyless go, wireless phone charging and heated seats.

The options list mirrors the Peugeot's down to the prices: sat-nav is £650, the load-through bulkhead £350 and an alarm £225, and the £850 camera pack is identical too. You sit a little lower than in some rivals, the plastics are hard-wearing rather than plush and the middle seat is tight.

### Which version to buy

The base van is well equipped, but add the alarm. Primo is the one if you want sat-nav, the alarm and the through-load bulkhead without ticking boxes. Servicing is every two years or 30,000 miles.

### Alternatives

Compare quotes on the twins; the Toyota is usually a bit dearer because of its better warranty. Our [ë-Dispatch](/reviews/citroen-dispatch/) and [E-Expert](/reviews/peugeot-expert/) reviews cover the closest siblings, and the [Ford E-Transit Custom](/vans/ford-transit-custom/) and [Kia PV5 Cargo](/vans/kia-pv5-cargo/) are the main rivals from outside the family.

### Price

We couldn't confirm a current UK list price before the grant. In March 2024 the E-Scudo was announced at £36,960 excluding VAT, but the reports don't make clear whether that was before or after the plug-in van grant (the E-Doblo price in the same report is after the grant). A 2025 review put the starting price at around £38,000, again without spelling out the basis. The E-Scudo is on the government's list of large vans eligible for the plug-in van grant, worth up to £5,000; our [grants guide](/guides/grants-and-tax/) explains how it works. The main warranty is three years or 100,000 miles, and the battery is covered for up to eight years.
