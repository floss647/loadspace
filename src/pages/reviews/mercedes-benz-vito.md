### Which eVito this is

Mercedes is working on a new generation of vans. Its VLE luxury people carrier sits on a new electric platform, and the next Vito will share some of the VLE's parts. In June 2026 the replacement was still described as upcoming. In October 2025 the earliest the new vans were expected was 2027, and the current Vito would stay on sale after the VLE launched. A July 2026 roundup of medium vans still lists the eVito as the electric choice in the Vito range, and the eVito is on the government's list of vans eligible for the plug-in van grant.

So this review covers the eVito panel van you can order now: the current model, facelifted in 2023, with a 66kWh pack with 60kWh usable. A 90kWh version with a much longer range exists, but Mercedes said it won't come to the UK. Few UK road tests of the current electric van have been published, so this is a shorter review built mainly on the published facts.

### Who it's for

Firms that already run diesel Vitos, or want the Mercedes badge and dealer network, and whose vans do predictable local rounds. If your days involve long motorway runs, look elsewhere.

### Load area and payload

The panel van comes in L2 and L3 lengths, with 6.0 and 6.6 cubic metres of load space, and the electric van loses nothing to the diesel. You get a lifting tailgate or twin barn doors at the back and a sliding door on each side. There's no high-roof version.

Payload is the catch, and the published figures don't agree. One report gives a maximum of 882kg, while a more recent roundup says 807kg. Either way it is well short of a tonne, and carrying anything near the maximum cuts the range. Check the plated payload of the exact van on your quote.

### Range and charging in the real world

Range figures differ slightly too. A February 2026 roundup quotes 162 miles on the official WLTP cycle, a July 2026 one says up to 160 miles on the WLTP combined cycle, and an earlier report said 159. That is modest for a medium electric van, and motorway speeds cut it significantly. One long-term road test found the official figure realistic in fair weather, with winter range noticeably lower.

Rapid charging peaks at 80kW, and a 10 to 80% top-up takes around 35 minutes. A 7kW wallbox takes just under 10 hours to fill it, so plan on overnight charging at home or the depot. For comparison, the 90kWh version sold elsewhere charges at up to 110kW. Our [charging guide](/guides/charging-an-electric-van/) explains the options.

### On the road

The most detailed UK road test of the electric van dates from January 2023, before the facelift, so treat what follows as reputation rather than a verdict on the latest van. By all accounts it picks up quickly from a standstill and is very refined at low speeds, but road noise becomes the main intrusion as speed rises. That test also noted a fair bit of road noise and found it could feel strained at 70mph. It also has a choice of driving modes and adjustable regenerative braking.

### Cab and kit

Expect Mercedes quirks: a foot-operated parking brake and a gear selector on the steering column, both of which take some getting used to. Road tests describe the cab as solidly built but on the cramped side. Reports of the current trim names differ, with some listing Progressive and Premium and one listing Pro and Select, so ask the dealer for the kit list. Mercedes vans come with free MobiloVan roadside assistance for up to 30 years if serviced by a Mercedes dealer.

### Alternatives

Most of the eVito's rivals go further on a charge and carry more.
