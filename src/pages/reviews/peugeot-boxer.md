The E-Boxer is Peugeot's version of the big Stellantis electric van. Like the [Vauxhall Movano Electric](/vans/vauxhall-movano/), [Citroën ë-Relay](/vans/citroen-relay/), [Fiat E-Ducato](/vans/fiat-ducato/) and [Toyota Proace Max Electric](/vans/toyota-proace-max/), it is the same van with a different face, and it has the same motor and battery. Before an update at the end of 2024, Peugeot's electric Boxer was converted by an outside company, so don't judge the current van by older reviews. We found no detailed UK road test of the current electric van on its own: the one detailed review we found covers the whole Boxer range, diesel included.

### Who it's for

Fleets and trades who want a big electric van and a simple choice. There's one trim, Professional, and it comes in four panel van sizes: L3H2, L3H3, L4H2 and L4H3. If you want a factory conversion, Fiat's Ducato offers more choice.

### Load area and payload

Payload is the figure to check. Some versions carry under 700kg, and the heavy-duty versions carry up to 1,385kg. The same van with other badges is quoted as 1,460kg, so ask for the plated weight of the exact van you're ordering. If you're looking at a heavier version, note that under the current GOV.UK rules a standard category B car licence covers electric vehicles up to 4,250kg, including one gained on or after 1 January 1997 (the limit for petrol and diesel vans is 3,500kg). It can tow up to 2,400kg, but Peugeot doesn't offer a tow bar on the electric van, so you'll need an aftermarket fitting.

### Range and charging

The official range is 261 miles. Rapid charging at up to 150kW takes it to 80% in 55 minutes. With an 11kW on-board charger, Peugeot quoted a full charge in 12 hours, while a 7.4kW wallbox needs around 17 hours. When it is heavily loaded, expect it to be best kept to shorter trips and urban work. More in our [charging guide](/guides/charging-an-electric-van/).

### On the road

This is where the E-Boxer earns its place, according to the one detailed review we found of the Boxer range. It rates the E-Boxer the best Boxer to drive by far. The motor is smooth, quiet and strong, and the battery weight makes it more comfortable when empty than the diesel. The design is old, though, and wind noise builds at motorway speeds. A reversing camera is standard, which helps with a van this size.

### Cab and kit

Standard kit includes air conditioning, rear parking sensors, cruise control and electric door mirrors, plus a digital instrument display. Check the screen size and options on the spec of the van you're quoted.

### Warranty and price

You get three years or 100,000 miles on the van and a battery covered for eight years against loss of capacity. Among the twins, the Toyota Proace Max Electric has longer cover.

We couldn't confirm a current UK list price. Peugeot's launch prices, published in March 2024, ran from £49,485 excluding VAT for the L3H2 listed as the "435" version to £51,915 for the L4H3 listed as a "440" version, both before the grant. In June 2025, prices were reported to start at over £50,000 before the grant. The E-Boxer is on the government's list of large vans eligible for the van grant, worth up to £5,000 (see our [grants and tax guide](/guides/grants-and-tax/)). Our [electric vs diesel calculator](/tools/electric-vs-diesel/) shows whether it pays for your mileage.

### Alternatives

Price up the four twins first. Outside the family, try the [Renault Master E-Tech](/vans/renault-master/), [Ford E-Transit](/vans/ford-transit/) and [Mercedes-Benz eSprinter](/vans/mercedes-benz-sprinter/).
