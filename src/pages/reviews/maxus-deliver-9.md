The Maxus eDeliver 9 is the brand's large electric van, the battery version of the diesel Deliver 9. It has been around since orders opened in December 2020 and has a strong following among delivery fleets: Tesco ordered 750 of them in November 2025, with deliveries starting from February 2026.

### What we could and couldn't confirm

Maxus's own UK site was not reachable for us, and the battery line-up has changed since most of the published road tests. At launch there were three batteries, 51.5kWh, 72kWh and 88.55kWh, and the biggest was rated at 185 miles on the WLTP combined cycle. A November 2025 fleet news report on the Tesco order described a 100kWh battery and a WLTP range of up to 219 miles, with rapid charging at 120kW, but the same report goes on to describe the chassis cab, and it doesn't say which bodies those figures cover. At launch, 219 miles was its WLTP city figure, not its combined figure, and published rapid charging figures give a peak of 90kW for the launch batteries. So we've left range, charging, payload and price unconfirmed. Ask the dealer for the current WLTP combined range and payload of the exact panel van you're ordering.

### Load area and payload

There are medium and long wheelbase panel vans, holding 9.7 and 11 cubic metres. Both are 2,525mm tall, so this is not one for low barriers. The battery sits under the load floor, so loads have to be lifted about 5cm higher than in the diesel Deliver 9. Payloads for the launch batteries ran from 1,200kg with the smallest battery in the medium wheelbase to 860kg with the biggest, but those figures are for the older line-up.

### On the road

The published UK road tests are of the launch-era van. By all accounts it's quick, with a 150kW motor, and the low-mounted batteries help it feel planted, with a decent ride. Reports agree there's more noise in the cab than you might expect. We'd expect much of this to carry over, but no road test of the updated batteries had been published that we could find.

### Cab and kit

You get one well-equipped trim, with a 10-inch touchscreen with Apple CarPlay and Android Auto. Cab storage is limited for a large van.

### Price

We couldn't find a UK price published in the last six months. In June 2023 it was reported from £63,000 excluding VAT and before the grant. It's on the government's list for the large-van rate of the Plug-in Van Grant, worth up to £5,000 (see our [grants guide](/guides/grants-and-tax/)). The van is covered for five years or 60,000 miles and the battery for eight years or 100,000 miles.
