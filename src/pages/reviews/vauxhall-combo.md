### Who it's for

The Combo Electric is for multi-drop couriers, service engineers and local trades whose days are mostly stop-start and whose vans come home to a charger. Vauxhall's version of the Stellantis small van, it shares its body and running gear with the [Citroën ë-Berlingo](/vans/citroen-berlingo/), [Peugeot E-Partner](/vans/peugeot-partner/), [Fiat E-Doblo](/vans/fiat-doblo/) and [Toyota Proace City Electric](/vans/toyota-proace-city/). We've based this review on the facelifted van, with figures from Vauxhall's November 2024 press kit and a review updated in August 2026.

### Load area and payload

Electric power takes nothing from the load bay. The standard van holds 3.3 cubic metres and the XL 3.9, and there's room for two Euro pallets in the back. The optional FlexCargo pack adds a bulkhead hatch for items up to 3,090mm long, or 3,440mm in the XL, plus a folding outer seat and a protective cargo bag.

Vauxhall's November 2024 press kit gave electric payloads of 642kg to 781kg, the lowest for the Crew Van. The van has had model-year updates since, and its Citroën and Peugeot twins now list a lower maximum for the panel van, so check the plated payload of the exact van you're quoted. The diesel carries more, and the electric van can only tow a 750kg braked trailer. If you're regularly near the limit, the diesel is still the better tool.

### Range and charging in the real world

Vauxhall's November 2024 press kit gave an official range of 213 miles (WLTP combined). The van has had model-year updates since, and official figures for the same van now vary with specification, so ask for the current figure for the exact van you're quoted. Vauxhall also gives a city figure of up to 304 miles, which tells you it is at its best in town. Motorway runs hurt its efficiency.

At a public rapid charger it takes up to 100kW, which means around half an hour to 80 per cent. A home wallbox refills it from empty overnight, and 11kW charging, which cuts that to around five hours, is an option on every trim. Our [charging guide](/guides/charging-an-electric-van/) has more on depot charging.

### On the road

The electric Combo has a reputation for being smooth and quiet. The most recent road test we've seen heard just a faint whine under hard acceleration, and found the low battery makes the ride smoother than the diesel's most of the time, though a heavy load can make the rear feel harsh over big bumps. Regenerative braking helps in traffic but is best switched off on the motorway, where it can feel abrupt each time you lift off.

### Cab and kit

This is where the Vauxhall differs, a little. You get the Astra-style steering wheel and stalks and Vauxhall's own screen graphics; otherwise it's the Citroën's cabin. The entry Prime trim has no touchscreen, just a phone dock and Bluetooth, though the facelift made rear parking sensors, air conditioning and cruise control standard. Prime-Plus adds a 10-inch touchscreen and a 10-inch digital instrument panel, and Pro adds navigation, a 4G connection and voice control.

Check the options list. The Drive Assist Pack, with adaptive cruise control and lane help, costs £1,250, and an alarm is standard on Pro and a £225 option otherwise.

### Which version to buy

Prime-Plus is the sensible middle, with the screen most drivers will want. Servicing is every two years or 25,000 miles, after a first inspection at one year or 12,500 miles. The van has a three-year, 100,000-mile warranty and the battery eight years or 100,000 miles.

### Alternatives

Its four siblings are the first comparison, and the Toyota stands out with up to 10 years of cover if it's serviced by Toyota. Beyond the family, look at the [Renault Kangoo E-Tech](/vans/renault-kangoo/) and [Nissan Townstar](/vans/nissan-townstar/), and note that the Kia PV5 Cargo sells for a very similar price despite being bigger: see our [PV5 review](/reviews/kia-pv5-cargo/).
