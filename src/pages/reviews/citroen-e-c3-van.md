The ë-C3 Van is on sale in the UK as a car-derived van: it's the ë-C3 electric hatchback with the rear seats taken out and a bulkhead put in, based on the Plus version of the ë-C3 hatchback. There is one body and one battery. Few UK road tests of it have been published, so this is a shorter review built mainly from published facts.

### Who it's for

Sole traders, engineers, carers and city couriers who carry tools, samples or small parcels and want something that drives and parks like a small car. If you carry anything heavy, look elsewhere.

### Load area and payload

The load bay is 1.2m long and 1m wide at floor level, with 1,220 litres of space, or 730 litres if you keep things hidden under the load cover. You get a full-height mesh and solid bulkhead, the rear door glass is blanked out, and lashing eyes are standard. Access is through two rear side doors as well as the tailgate.

The weak point is payload: 300kg, and that figure includes a 75kg driver. A pre-launch report in March 2025 gave 494kg, but the launch figures and road test since all say 300kg, so plan on that.

### Range and charging in the real world

The 44kWh battery and 113hp motor give an official 200 miles, which is strong for this class. Rapid charging runs at up to 100kW, taking the battery from 20 to 80 per cent in around 26 minutes, and the onboard AC charger is 7.4kW, single-phase. We couldn't find a measured real-world range figure. Our [charging guide](/guides/charging-an-electric-van/) covers home and depot charging.

### On the road

The one UK road test we could read found it easy and pleasant to drive, and comfortable. The gripes were small: regenerative braking is boosted through the gear selector rather than steering wheel paddles, and the dash shows a charge percentage rather than a miles-remaining readout.

### Cab and kit

Standard kit is generous for the money: a 10.25-inch touchscreen with wireless Apple CarPlay and Android Auto, LED headlights, cruise control, rear parking sensors and 17-inch steel wheels with trims, which are cheaper to replace if kerbed. There's no built-in sat nav, so you'll use your phone, and the head-up display is really a driver display mounted high on the dashboard. It has good cab storage.

### Alternatives

The obvious rival is the Dacia Spring Cargo, which is cheaper at £14,995 before the grant but has a 26.8kWh battery and an official range of 140 miles. If you need more payload, step up to the [Citroën ë-Berlingo](/vans/citroen-berlingo/) or [Ford E-Transit Courier](/vans/ford-transit-courier/).

### Price

We couldn't confirm a current UK list price. At launch in June 2025 it was reported from £22,437, or £19,937 after the grant, described as an on-the-road price, without saying whether VAT was included. A pre-launch report put it at £15,035 with VAT removed and the grant taken off. Those figures are old and on unclear bases, and a cheaper version with a smaller battery was promised, so get a written quote. It's on the government's approved list for the small-van grant, worth up to £2,500 (see our [grants and tax guide](/guides/grants-and-tax/)).
