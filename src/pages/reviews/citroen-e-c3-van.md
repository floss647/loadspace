The ë-C3 Van is on sale in the UK as a car-derived van: it's the ë-C3 electric hatchback with the rear seats taken out and a bulkhead put in, based on the Plus version of the ë-C3 hatchback. It launched as a single version, with a 44kWh battery. We couldn't find a road test of the van on a source we could check, so this is a shorter review built from published facts.

### Who it's for

Sole traders, engineers, carers and city couriers who carry tools, samples or small parcels and want something that drives and parks like a small car. If you carry anything heavy, look elsewhere.

### Load area and payload

The load bay is 1.2m long and 1m wide at floor level, with 1,220 litres of space, or 730 litres if you keep things hidden under the load cover. You get a full-height mesh and solid bulkhead, and the rear windows are opaque and don't open.

The weak point is the payload, which is 300kg, so weigh what you carry before you buy. A pre-launch report in March 2025 gave 494kg, but the launch figures since say 300kg, so plan on that.

### Range and charging in the real world

The 44kWh battery and 113hp motor give an official 200 miles, which is strong for this class. Rapid charging runs at up to 100kW, taking the battery from 20 to 80 per cent in around 26 minutes. On a 7.4kW AC supply, a 20 to 80 per cent charge is listed at around 250 minutes, so an overnight top-up at home or the depot is easy. We couldn't find a measured real-world range figure. Our [charging guide](/guides/charging-an-electric-van/) covers home and depot charging.

### On the road

The van keeps the same seats and Advanced Comfort suspension as the hatchback. One launch report said its reviewers had found the hatchback a little underpowered, adding that this matters less for urban deliveries. If you can, drive one on your usual routes before you buy.

### Cab and kit

Standard kit is generous for the money: a 10.25-inch touchscreen with wireless Apple CarPlay and Android Auto, a head-up display, LED headlights, cruise control, rear parking sensors and 17-inch steel wheels with trims, which are cheaper to refurbish if kerbed. Cab storage includes door pockets, a closed glovebox and a high centre console with closed storage.

### Alternatives

The obvious rival is the Dacia Spring Cargo. Reports in 2025 put it at £14,995, with a payload of up to 370kg and a claimed range of 140 miles, so check its current price and spec. If the payload is too small for you, compare purpose-built small vans such as the [Citroën ë-Berlingo](/vans/citroen-berlingo/) or [Ford E-Transit Courier](/vans/ford-transit-courier/).

### Price

We couldn't confirm a current UK list price. At launch in June 2025 it was reported from £22,437, or £19,937 after the grant. One launch report described it as an on-the-road price and another as excluding VAT. A pre-launch report suggested it could cost from £15,035 with VAT removed and the grant taken off. Those figures are old and on unclear bases, so get a written quote. It's on the government's approved list for the small-van grant, worth up to £2,500 (see our [grants and tax guide](/guides/grants-and-tax/)).
