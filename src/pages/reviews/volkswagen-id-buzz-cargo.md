The ID. Buzz Cargo is the van that gets noticed: a modern electric take on Volkswagen's classic bus, and the winner of the Auto Express Electric Van of the Year 2023 award. It shares its body with the ID. Buzz passenger MPV, but this review covers the panel van only.

### Who it's for

Businesses that carry light, bulky loads and want a van that does some of their marketing for them. UK buyers currently get one body length, though a longer version has been announced (see below).

### Load area and payload

The load bay holds 3.9 cubic metres, with room for two Euro pallets loaded widthways. The floor is 2,208mm long with the tailgate or 2,232mm with twin rear doors, and 1,217mm wide, with little wheelarch intrusion because the floor sits high over the battery. The price of that is a rear load lip that is high at 632mm, and the twin sliding side doors open to just 756mm. There's no through-loading for long lengths, and it tows one tonne.

Payload is the real weakness. A July 2026 buying guide gave a maximum payload of 712kg, and the 59kWh van launched in 2025 was rated at 692kg. We couldn't find a payload figure for the new 58kWh van. For comparison, the [Kia PV5 Cargo](/reviews/kia-pv5-cargo/) offers 4.4 cubic metres and a maximum payload of 790kg.

### Range and charging in the real world

There are two batteries. In August 2026 a 58kWh battery replaced the 59kWh in the entry Commerce van, and its official range rose to 218 miles, 17 miles more than before. It charges at up to 105kW, with a 10 to 80 per cent top-up taking 26 minutes, whereas the 59kWh battery it replaced could take 165kW.

Commerce Plus keeps the 79kWh battery (early vans had a 77kWh battery), rated at up to 276 miles on the official WLTP combined test (some reports give 277). It rapid charges at up to 185kW, enough for five to 80 per cent in around half an hour. We couldn't find a measured real-world range for the current batteries. ### On the road

The published road tests we found were of the earlier 77kWh van with the original 201bhp motor, not today's 58kWh or 79kWh versions. On that evidence the ID. Buzz Cargo has a reputation for being one of the most car-like vans to drive: smooth, quiet on the move, with good visibility and a tight turning circle. One test found that bigger bumps can send a shudder through the body, especially when it's empty. Power runs from 188bhp in the entry van to 282bhp in the 79kWh van and 335bhp 4Motion all-wheel drive.

### Cab and kit

The Cargo's cab is plainer than the MPV's, with darker, harder plastics. The low roof makes the cab feel tight, and the touch-sensitive controls, including the climate control, are widely criticised. Commerce comes with a 12.9-inch touchscreen, Apple CarPlay and Android Auto, climate control, a heated driver's seat and front and rear parking sensors. Commerce Plus adds navigation, a heated windscreen, keyless entry, adaptive cruise control and a rear-view camera. Since the August update every version gets vehicle-to-load (V2L) power at up to 3.6kW, and the driver assistance can stop for red traffic lights.

### Which version to buy

The 58kWh Commerce is the one to buy for most businesses: it's the cheapest, and 218 miles is plenty for local work. Pick Commerce Plus only if you regularly cover long distances. Every version has a three-year, 100,000-mile warranty, and the battery is covered for eight years or 100,000 miles.

If you need more room, it may be worth waiting. In September 2026 Volkswagen announced a long-wheelbase Cargo, on order in Germany, with a load area 250mm longer (4.4 cubic metres in all), a payload of 700kg (single motor) or 606kg (dual motor), and side doors 190mm longer, so a full-size Euro pallet fits through. It comes only with the largest battery and brings back physical steering wheel buttons. It's due in the UK soon at a reported premium of around £2,000, but UK prices haven't been confirmed.
