verdict: "The E-Expert is a capable, well-equipped electric medium van with an official range of up to 230 miles and 100kW rapid charging. Peugeot's version stands out for its trims: Professional is well stocked, Asphalt adds the cameras, sat-nav and through-load bulkhead, and Sport is there if you want to be noticed. Payload is a healthy 1,210kg in the M panel van, but the cargo bay trails the biggest rivals and the cab is tight for three. Under the badge it's the same van as the Citroën, Vauxhall, Fiat and Toyota, so shop around."

### Who it's for

Peugeot's electric medium van makes most sense for businesses doing town and regional work who want a well-kitted cab. Strip off the lion badge and it's the same van as the [Citroën ë-Dispatch](/vans/citroen-dispatch/), [Vauxhall Vivaro Electric](/vans/vauxhall-vivaro/), [Fiat E-Scudo](/vans/fiat-scudo/) and [Toyota Proace Electric](/vans/toyota-proace/): the bodywork, battery and motor are shared, and only the styling and badges really change.

### Load area and payload

There are two lengths, M and XL, with 5.3 and 6.1 cubic metres of space. On Asphalt and Sport panel vans, a hatch in the bulkhead lets long items slide under the folding outer passenger seat, stretching the floor length of an M from 2,512mm to 3,674mm. There are six load tie-down hooks in the M and eight in the XL, and Professional panel vans have a full-height steel bulkhead.

Peugeot's October 2026 price list settles the payload question. It gives up to 1,210kg for the M panel van and 1,138kg for the XL. These are worked out from a kerb weight without a driver, so the driver's weight comes out of them. The electric crew vans carry 1,075kg (M) and 1,011kg (XL), while the best diesel panel van manages 1,326kg. Peugeot says its payloads don't allow for options, so check the plated weight of the exact van you're ordering. The electric van is rated to tow 1,000kg, but the only tow bar on Peugeot's options list is for the diesel.

### Range and charging in the real world

The 50kWh battery went at the 2024 facelift, so every E-Expert has the 75kWh pack, of which 69kWh is usable. Peugeot's current price list gives an official range of 194 to 230 miles, depending on the options fitted, though its technical page quotes 219 miles. At its March 2024 launch the figure was 217 miles. One road test of a Long van in Asphalt trim reported getting very close to 230 miles in cold weather with some weight aboard, but motorway driving cuts range noticeably.

From 5 to 80% on a 100kW charger takes 45 minutes. An 11kW on-board charger is standard on every trim, and a three-phase 11kW wallbox does a full charge in 7 hours 30 minutes, while a 7.4kW wallbox takes 11 hours 20 minutes. A domestic plug takes 36 hours 45 minutes, so don't rely on one. See our [charging guide](/guides/charging-an-electric-van/) for wallbox advice.

### On the road

Both road tests we found describe a quiet cab, though one notes wind and road noise at motorway speeds. That test found its 134bhp fine away from the lights but short when you want to overtake on the motorway. Reports on the ride differ. One account says the battery's weight keeps it comfortable nearly all the time; the other found the ride under-damped, with bumping and thumping from the tyres and suspension the main noise in an otherwise hushed cab. The first test also says the strongest regen setting slows you hard enough to switch on the brake lights, and that the door mirrors are the weak point for visibility.

### Cab and kit

There are three trims: Professional, Asphalt and a limited-edition Sport, each offered as an M or XL panel van or crew van. Professional gets two 10-inch screens, wireless phone connection, sliding doors on both sides, a full steel bulkhead, air conditioning, parking sensors, lane keep assist and auto lights and wipers. Asphalt costs £2,675 more and adds the through-load bulkhead, TomTom sat-nav, 17-inch wheels, an alarm, a digital rear-view mirror, front sensors, blind-spot sensors and a reversing camera with a bird's-eye view. Sport gets a rally-style body kit and graphics, 18-inch alloy wheels, eco-leather seats, dual-zone climate control, a heated driver's seat, a heated steering wheel and wireless phone charging, though it loses the digital rear-view mirror. On Professional and Asphalt, a heated driver's seat comes in a £500 Winter Pack, with a heated steering wheel and extra airbags.

The 'OK Peugeot' voice assistant now uses ChatGPT, but the system is complicated enough to leave some drivers baffled for a while. The climate controls stay as physical buttons and dials. You sit lower than in a Ford Transit Custom or Renault Trafic, and the middle seat is narrow.

### Which version to buy

Asphalt makes sense if you want the cameras and the load-through bulkhead. Adding sat-nav (£650), the Moduwork load-through bulkhead (£350), an alarm (£225) and Dynamic Surround View (£750) to a Professional comes to £1,975, against the £2,675 price gap, but Asphalt also brings LED headlights, front fog lights, 17-inch wheels and painted bumpers. Adaptive cruise control with Stop and Go is a £600 option on Professional. These option prices are, like the list prices, all excluding VAT. Servicing is every two years or 25,000 miles, with a first check at one year or 12,500 miles.

### Alternatives

Among the twins, the Toyota has the better warranty and tends to cost more. ### Price

Peugeot's price list from 8 October 2026 starts the E-Expert at £41,460 excluding VAT, for the M panel van in Professional trim, before the £5,000 plug-in van grant. The XL Professional is £42,260, the Asphalt panel vans £44,135 and £44,935, and the Sport panel vans £52,095 and £52,895, all excluding VAT and before the grant. Crew vans start at £44,160. The E-Expert is on the government's list of large vans eligible for the plug-in van grant, worth up to £5,000 (see our [grants guide](/guides/grants-and-tax/)). The main warranty is three years or 100,000 miles, and the battery is covered for eight years or 100,000 miles, to 70% of its capacity.
