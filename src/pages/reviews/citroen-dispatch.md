### Who it's for

The ë-Dispatch is Citroën's badge on the Stellantis medium electric van. It shares its body and electric drivetrain with the [Vauxhall Vivaro Electric](/vans/vauxhall-vivaro/), [Peugeot E-Expert](/vans/peugeot-expert/), [Fiat E-Scudo](/vans/fiat-scudo/) and [Toyota Proace Electric](/vans/toyota-proace/), and the differences come down to small design changes to the nose and the cab. So the real choice is about price, kit and which dealer is closest. It suits local and regional work. Our [Vivaro Electric review](/reviews/vauxhall-vivaro/) covers the shared basics from Vauxhall's side.

### Load area and payload

The compact version was dropped in the 2024 facelift, leaving two lengths, M and XL. The M holds 5.3 cubic metres and the XL 6.1, and both share a 3,275mm wheelbase, with the XL's extra 350mm all in the rear overhang. The EXTENSO load-through bulkhead, standard on Driver and VTR trims, stretches the floor length of an M from 2,512mm to 3,674mm. The sliding side doors are 935mm wide, small for this class.

Citroën's October 2026 price list settles the payload question. It gives up to 1,210kg for the M panel van and 1,138kg for the XL. These are worked out from a kerb weight without a driver, so the driver's weight comes out of them. The electric crew vans carry 1,075kg (M) and 1,011kg (XL). Options add weight, so check the plated weight of the exact van you're ordering. The electric van is rated to tow 1,000kg, but the only tow bar on Citroën's options list is for the diesel.

### Range and charging in the real world

The 50kWh battery was dropped in 2024, so every ë-Dispatch now has a 75kWh pack, of which 69kWh is usable. Citroën's current price list gives an official range of 194 to 230 miles, though its technical page quotes 219 miles. Speed matters: at a steady 70mph you can expect to lose around two miles of range for every seven or eight you drive.

A 100kW rapid charger takes it from 5 to 80% in 45 minutes, and a 7.4kW wallbox fills it in 11 hours 20 minutes. An 11kW on-board charger is now standard on every trim, and on a three-phase 11kW wallbox a full charge takes 7 hours 30 minutes. Our [charging guide](/guides/charging-an-electric-van/) explains the choices.

### On the road

Driving impressions here come from one published road test. It found the electric van quiet and refined. Its 134bhp motor feels keen off the line but has little more to give once you're moving. The battery sits low under the load floor, which the test credits with a good ride, though wind and road noise are noticeable at motorway speeds. Paddles behind the wheel give three levels of regenerative braking, and the strongest setting is well suited to town work, though it won't bring the van to a stop on its own. Rear visibility could be better, as there are no wide-angle mirrors.

### Cab and kit

There are three trims: Enterprise, Driver and a limited-edition VTR. Driver costs £2,675 more than Enterprise. Enterprise has twin sliding doors, a full steel bulkhead, air conditioning, rear parking sensors, auto lights and wipers, a 10-inch driver's display and a 10-inch touchscreen with wireless phone connection. Driver adds the EXTENSO folding passenger seat and load-through bulkhead, a Thatcham category 1 alarm, LED headlights, front parking sensors, a 180-degree reversing camera, a digital rear-view mirror, blind-spot detection and sat-nav. VTR adds eco-leather seats, 18-inch alloy wheels, dual-zone climate control, keyless entry, wireless phone charging and a heated steering wheel and driver's seat. One option that suits trades is a £350 power take-off, which lets you run tools from the drive battery; Citroën lists it for the Enterprise panel van.

The heating and ventilation keep their own physical controls. But you sit lower than in most rivals, and the cab is narrow if you regularly carry two passengers.

### Which version to buy

An Enterprise M covers most needs, but on that trim the alarm is a £225 option, and we'd tick it. Adaptive cruise control is a £600 option on Enterprise, and an electric parking brake is standard. Servicing is every two years or 25,000 miles, with a first check at one year or 12,500 miles.

### Alternatives

Get quotes on the twins: the Toyota costs slightly more but adds 12 months of warranty with every service, for up to 10 years. Outside the family, compare the [Ford E-Transit Custom](/vans/ford-transit-custom/) and the [Kia PV5 Cargo](/vans/kia-pv5-cargo/).

### Price

Citroën's price list from 8 October 2026 starts the ë-Dispatch at £41,460 excluding VAT, for the M panel van in Enterprise trim, before the £5,000 plug-in van grant. The XL Enterprise is £42,260, the Driver panel vans £44,135 and £44,935, and the VTR panel vans £52,095 and £52,895, all excluding VAT and before the grant. The ë-Dispatch is on the government's list of large vans eligible for the plug-in van grant, which is worth up to £5,000; our [grants guide](/guides/grants-and-tax/) has the detail. The van warranty is three years or 100,000 miles, and the battery and running gear are covered for eight years and 100,000 miles.
