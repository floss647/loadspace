### Who it's for

The ë-Dispatch is Citroën's badge on the Stellantis medium electric van. It shares its body and electric drivetrain with the [Vauxhall Vivaro Electric](/vans/vauxhall-vivaro/), [Peugeot E-Expert](/vans/peugeot-expert/), [Fiat E-Scudo](/vans/fiat-scudo/) and [Toyota Proace Electric](/vans/toyota-proace/), and the differences come down to small design changes to the nose and the cab. So the real choice is about price, kit and which dealer is closest. It suits local and regional work. Our [Vivaro Electric review](/reviews/vauxhall-vivaro/) covers the shared basics from Vauxhall's side.

### Load area and payload

The compact version was dropped in the 2024 facelift, leaving two lengths, M and XL. The M holds 5.3 cubic metres and the XL 6.1, and both share a 3,275mm wheelbase, with the XL's extra 350mm all in the rear overhang. The Moduwork through-load bulkhead, standard on Driver trim, stretches the floor length of an M from 2,512mm to 3,674mm. The sliding side doors are 935mm wide, small for this class.

Payload is hard to pin down. At its March 2024 launch Citroën quoted 1,000kg for the electric panel van, and a July 2026 buyers' list gives the same maximum, but a 2025 review says every standard van, electric versions included, carries more than a tonne, and the Vauxhall twin has been reported at up to 1,210kg. The electric XL Crew Van is lowest, at 915kg. Check the plated weight of the exact van you're ordering. The electric van is rated to tow a tonne, but the only tow bar on Citroën's options list is for the diesel.

### Range and charging in the real world

The 50kWh battery was dropped in 2024, so every ë-Dispatch now has a 75kWh pack. Published range figures differ: the March 2024 launch figure was 217 miles, a 2025 review gives Citroën's combined figure as 195 to 230 miles, and a July 2026 buyers' list puts it at up to 231 miles, citing Citroën. The heavier XL is less likely to get near the top figure, and speed matters: at a steady 70mph you can expect to lose around two miles of range for every seven or eight you drive.

A 100kW rapid charger takes it from 5 to 80% in 45 minutes, and a 7.4kW wallbox fills it in 11 hours 20 minutes. The 11kW on-board charger is a £400 option. Our [charging guide](/guides/charging-an-electric-van/) explains the choices.

### On the road

Driving impressions here come from one published road test. It found the electric van quiet and refined. Its 134bhp motor feels keen off the line but has little more to give once you're moving. The battery sits low under the load floor, which the test credits with a good ride, though wind and road noise are noticeable at motorway speeds. Paddles behind the wheel give three levels of regenerative braking, and the strongest setting is well suited to town work, though it won't bring the van to a stop on its own. Rear visibility could be better, as there are no wide-angle mirrors.

### Cab and kit

There are two trims, Enterprise and Driver, with around £2,700 between them. Enterprise has twin sliding doors, a full steel bulkhead, air conditioning, rear parking sensors, auto lights and wipers, a 10-inch driver's display and a 10-inch touchscreen with wireless phone connection. Driver adds the Moduwork through-load bulkhead, a Thatcham category 1 alarm, LED headlights, front parking sensors, a 180-degree reversing camera, a digital rear-view mirror, blind-spot detection and sat-nav. One option that suits trades is a £350 power take-off, which lets you run tools from the drive battery.

The heating and ventilation keep their own physical controls. But you sit lower than in most rivals, and the cab is narrow if you regularly carry two passengers.

### Which version to buy

An Enterprise M covers most needs, but on that trim the alarm is a £225 option, and we'd tick it. The Drive Assist Pack (adaptive cruise control and an electric parking brake) is £950. Servicing is every two years or 30,000 miles.

### Alternatives

Get quotes on the twins: the Toyota costs slightly more but adds 12 months of warranty with every service, for up to 10 years. Outside the family, compare the [Ford E-Transit Custom](/vans/ford-transit-custom/) and the [Kia PV5 Cargo](/vans/kia-pv5-cargo/).

### Price

We couldn't confirm a current UK list price before the grant, so get a written quote. At its March 2024 launch the ë-Dispatch started from £36,210 excluding VAT and after the plug-in van grant. A buyers' list in July 2026 gave £37,699, again excluding VAT and after the grant. A May 2025 review quoted £41,200 before any grants or discounts but didn't say whether that included VAT. The ë-Dispatch is on the government's list of large vans eligible for the plug-in van grant, which is worth up to £5,000; our [grants guide](/guides/grants-and-tax/) has the detail. The van warranty is three years or 100,000 miles, and the battery and running gear are covered for eight years and 100,000 miles.
