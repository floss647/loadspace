## The short answer

An electric van usually makes sense if **all three** of these are true:

1. You do **under about 100 miles on a normal working day**, or you can top up during the day.
2. You can **charge off-street overnight**, at home or at a yard.
3. You keep the van **long enough to benefit** from lower running costs, or you lease it so falling values aren't your problem.

If you do long motorway days, tow heavy loads, or would rely on public rapid chargers, diesel is still often the better choice for now.

## The four numbers that decide it

| Number | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Your longest regular day (miles)** | Real-world range drops with a full load and in winter. Plan on well under the official figure. |
| **Where you'll charge** | Home or depot electricity is a fraction of diesel per mile. Public rapid charging can cost several times more and wipes out most of the saving. |
| **Your annual miles** | The more miles you do, the more the lower running costs pay back. |
| **How you pay for it** | Leasing new protects you from falling values. Buying used is cheap but you carry the battery risk. |

## What you save

- **Fuel:** often 2–4p a mile on an off-peak home tariff, against roughly 15–20p a mile for diesel.
- **Servicing:** no clutch, diesel particulate filter, AdBlue or timing belt. Brakes last longer.
- **Company van tax:** zero-emission vans have no taxable van benefit when drivers take them home (check current rules on GOV.UK).
- **London Congestion Charge:** electric vans registered for Auto Pay pay 50% (£9 a day, not £18).

## What it costs you

- **A higher price new**, partly offset by the government van grant on eligible new vans.
- **Less payload** in many models, because batteries are heavy.
- **Road tax:** electric vans have paid standard van road tax since April 2025.
- **A home or depot charger** if you don't have one.
- **Time,** if you have to charge away from base.
