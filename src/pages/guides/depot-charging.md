## Why start early

For most fleets the hardest part isn't the vehicles, it's **power**. Adding chargers can mean a bigger grid connection. The motor industry body SMMT says waits for large grid connections can stretch to years. Talk to your network operator before you order vehicles.

## Step 1: Work out how much energy you need

For each vehicle: **daily miles ÷ miles per kWh = kWh needed per day.** Add up the fleet and allow a margin for winter and heavy loads.

## Step 2: Work out how fast you need it

Divide each vehicle's daily kWh by the hours it's parked. Vans parked for 10+ hours overnight usually manage with 7–22kW chargers. Trucks, double shifts and short turnarounds need rapid chargers.

## Step 3: Check your site's supply

Your electrician or a charging company can check your current capacity. If it isn't enough:
- **Smart charging** spreads the load so you need less peak power.
- **Staggered schedules** charge vehicles in turns.
- **Batteries or solar** on site can help in some cases.
- **A new or upgraded grid connection** is the longest step.

## Step 4: Choose chargers and software

- Make sure charger power matches what each vehicle can take.
- Use charging software to schedule charging for cheap overnight rates and to report energy use per vehicle.
- Plan for growth: put in cabling for more chargers than you need on day one.

## Step 5: Drivers who take vans home

Home charging can be cheaper than depot charging for some fleets. You need a fair way to repay drivers for electricity, usually based on energy reported by the charger or the vehicle.

## Support

Grants for chargers have changed several times. Check GOV.UK and the Office for Zero Emission Vehicles for current schemes before you buy.
