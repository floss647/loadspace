## Length and height codes

Most vans come in several lengths and roof heights, written as **L** (length) and **H** (height):

- **L1, L2, L3, L4:** short to extra-long wheelbase. Each step adds load length.
- **H1, H2, H3:** low, medium and high roof. H2 and above usually let most people stand up in the back.

So an **L2H2** is a long-wheelbase, medium-roof van, the most common choice for trades in large vans.

## Payload and gross weight

- **Gross vehicle weight (GVW):** the most the van can legally weigh, fully loaded with people and fuel. It's on a plate, usually inside the driver's door.
- **Payload:** what you can carry. Roughly the gross weight minus the van's own weight.

**Every fit-out eats payload.** Racking, ply lining, tail lifts and roof racks all count. If you carry heavy materials, check payload *after* conversion, not the brochure figure.

## What your car licence covers

- A standard car licence (category B) covers vans up to **3.5 tonnes** gross weight.
- **Electric vans** can be driven on a car licence up to **4.25 tonnes**, because batteries are heavy. The extra training requirement was removed in 2024.
- If you passed your test **before 1 January 1997**, you probably also hold category C1, covering vehicles up to 7.5 tonnes. Check your licence.

## Will a sheet of plywood fit?

A standard sheet is **2440 × 1220 mm**. Two things matter: **load length** (2.44m+) and **width between the wheel arches** (1.22m+). Many medium vans manage the width; length depends on wheelbase and whether there's a through-load hatch in the bulkhead. Check both figures for the exact version.
