## 1. Know what your vehicles actually do

Pull a few months of telematics or fuel card data for every vehicle: **daily miles, longest days, payload, where it parks overnight, and how long it's parked.** This tells you which vehicles can switch now and which can't yet.

## 2. Pick the easy wins first

The best first electric vehicles are those with **predictable, short-to-medium daily miles that return to the same place every night.** Start with a pilot of a few vehicles, not the whole fleet.

## 3. Compare total cost, not the price tag

Compare electric and diesel over the life of the vehicle: lease or purchase cost after grants, energy, servicing, tax and charges, and resale. [Fleet switch calculator](/tools/fleet-cost/)

## 4. Sort out charging early

Depot power is often the slowest part. Check your capacity and talk to your network operator before ordering vehicles. [Depot charging guide](/guides/depot-charging/)

## 5. Choose how to fund it

Leasing is common for electric vans because the leasing company takes the risk on resale values. Some funders also bundle chargers. [Lease, buy new or buy used?](/tools/lease-or-buy/)

## 6. Bring drivers with you

- Train drivers on range, regenerative braking and charging.
- Agree a fair home-charging repayment for drivers who take vans home.
- Share early results, like cost per mile and reliability, so the rest of the team sees the benefit.

## 7. Measure and expand

Track cost per mile, uptime and range in real conditions. Expand in waves as vehicles come up for renewal.

## Rules that help fleets

- **Zero-emission vans have no van benefit tax** for drivers who take them home.
- **Car licence holders can drive electric vans up to 4.25 tonnes.**
- **Grants** are available on eligible new electric vans and trucks.
