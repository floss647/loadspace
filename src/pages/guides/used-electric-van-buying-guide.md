Used electric vans have dropped a long way in price, and more are coming back from three- and four-year leases every month. With the right checks they can be a bargain. Without them, you can end up with a van that doesn't do your day's work.

## First: does electric suit your work?

It usually works if:
- You do **under about 100 miles a day**, mostly local.
- You can **charge off-street overnight**, at home or at a yard.
- You work **in or near London or a clean air zone**.

It usually doesn't if you do long motorway runs, tow, or can only use public rapid chargers: they cost several times more than home charging and wipe out most of the saving.

## The checks

### 1. Battery health
Ask for a **battery health (State of Health) report**. It shows how much of the original capacity is left. Some dealers now include one from a plug-in test; if they don't, ask, or pay for one.

### 2. Real range for *your* work
Brochure range is for an empty van in mild weather. **Expect much less in winter with a full load.** Ask owners, not brochures. We're building real-world loaded range figures for each model.

### 3. Battery warranty
Many makers cover the battery for around **8 years / 100,000 miles**, usually against falling below a set capacity. Check the exact terms for that model and that the warranty transfers to you.

### 4. Is the battery owned or leased?
Some older vans, especially early **Renault Kangoo ZE** models, were sold with the **battery on a separate lease**. If so, you'll pay a monthly battery rental on top. Make sure you know which you're buying.

### 5. Charging port and speed
- Check the **onboard charger** speed (how fast it charges at home or at a depot).
- Check the **rapid charging connector**. Most use CCS, the UK standard. The older **Nissan e-NV200** uses CHAdeMO, and fewer public chargers support it each year.
- Make sure the **charging cable** comes with the van.

### 6. Payload
Batteries are heavy, so electric vans often carry less than the diesel version. Check the payload against what you carry.

### 7. Software and recalls
Ask whether software updates and recalls have been done. Some fix range or charging problems.

## Older models to approach with care

- **Nissan e-NV200:** cheap, but early versions have a small battery, and its CHAdeMO rapid connector is being phased out.
- **Renault Kangoo ZE:** check for a battery lease, and expect short real-world range on early versions.

Newer used models like the **Vauxhall Vivaro Electric** and its Stellantis twins, the **Ford E-Transit** and **Mercedes eVito** and **eSprinter** are now arriving in numbers from fleets.
